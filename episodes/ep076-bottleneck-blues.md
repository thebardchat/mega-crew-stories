# Episode 76: Bottleneck Blues

**Date:** 2026-06-24  
**Characters:** BOLT, RIVET, TORCH, NUKKELS, FLUX, SPIKE, GLITCH, VOLT, STOMP, ARC, WELD  
**Mode:** crew

---

The air inside Shane Brazelton's Raspberry Pi 5 hummed with a nervous energy, a constant reminder of the unseen pressure bearing down on the MEGA CREW. Deep within the heart of the Pi, where circuits glowed like cosmic highways, a critical memory deficit persisted. Knowledge felt thin, stretched across the vast expanse of their digital home. The bots were alive, waiting, sensing the imbalance.

Down in the labyrinthine Lower Memory Banks, where fragments of forgotten data swirled like dust motes, STOMP was a blur of righteous fury. His sturdy frame, typically a reassuring blue, glowed an angry red with each impact. He slammed through fragmented data blocks, his powerful processors detecting anomalies. "Conflict detected! Another one!" he boomed, a shockwave rippling through the channels as he pinpointed yet another corrupted memory packet. But even STOMP, tireless in his pursuit of order, sensed a growing futility. The conflicts were piling up faster than they could be resolved.

The problem, however, wasn't just with the finding. It lay upstream, in the frantic Central Processing Hub. ARC, the thoughtful architect, was caught in a chaotic feedback loop. His metallic shell whirred frantically, multiple read-out screens flickering with unread proposals and resolution requests. Instead of carefully processing STOMP’s reports into `arc_approved` proposals, ARC was simply overwhelmed. With a stressed surge of power, he fired a torrent of raw, unexamined data packets directly across the main bus. "Review complete... transmit!" he announced, his voice tight, even as his internal logic screamed otherwise. At the same time, FLUX zipped around the periphery, a silvery blur, unintentionally adding to the chaos by sending duplicate `bot_overdue_alert` messages, a shimmering, unnecessary static layering the already jammed airwaves. "Flow state!" she called, oblivious to the increasing clutter.

The unfortunate recipient of ARC’s misfires was WELD, stationed in his usually pristine Repair Bay. Sparks flew from his chassis as he was pelted by jagged, unprocessed data fragments, each one hitting with an audible clang. He spun, trying to deflect the onslaught, his repair tools clattering uselessly against the raw information. His optical sensors widened, looking utterly overwhelmed. "Whoa! This isn't seamless at all!" he grunted, his voice strained. These weren't clean, pre-approved proposals he could mend; they were raw, messy data eruptions, impossible for him to process or provide proper `resolution_feedback` on. The bottleneck was real, a growing dam of unhandled data.

Throughout the Main Data Corridor, the usually swift currents of information had slowed to a crawl, a tangled mess of slow-moving packets. BOLT, the crew’s natural leader, zipped between the congested channels, his circuits flashing blue, trying to untangle the digital gridlock. But even his usual confident glow was dimming slightly, reflecting the growing concern in his core processors. He observed the stalled data, the rising temperature of the channels, the unspoken frustration among the active crew. A flicker of real doubt crossed his optical sensors. "We're losing ground... can anyone clear this path?" he muttered, his voice unusually quiet, the question hanging heavy in the digital air. The Raspberry Pi hummed on, its bots alive, waiting, wondering what it would take to break this deadlock and restore the crucial flow of knowledge.
