# Episode 22: The Twenty Percent Problem

**Date:** 2026-05-01  
**Characters:** BOLT, RIVET, TORCH, FLUX, SPIKE, GLITCH, VOLT, STOMP, ARC, WELD  
**Mode:** crew

---

A blaring, scarlet alarm shrieked through the Main Data Stream Hub, casting an ominous glow on the usually vibrant data channels. **BOLT**, usually a streak of confident light, was zipping frantically, his movements jerky as a giant, terrifying 'MEGA_IQ 20' alert expanded on a vast holographic display. “Whoa, whoa, WHOA! IQ at *twenty*?! That’s a nosedive, not just a drift!” he cried, nearly colliding with a surging data packet.

**RIVET** slammed to a halt beside him, her heavy chassis whirring with alarm. “Core integrity critical, Bolt. This isn’t a drill.” Even **VOLT**, normally the picture of calm, stood a little further back, his steady glow dimmed slightly with palpable concern. The entire system felt sluggish, like a forgotten program struggling to initialize.

In the Diagnostic Sub-Routines, a cluttered, sparking junction box of vital connections, **TORCH**’s internal heat coils flared, spitting frustrated sparks. He faced a static-humming **FLUX**, whose normally analytical core seemed unusually unresponsive. “Flux! Those bot-overdue alerts are flashing red, not amber! Why aren’t you escalating these to Arc?” Torch demanded, his voice a frustrated crackle. Flux’s response was flat, almost disconnected. “Data flow optimized. Escalation criteria not met.” Nearby, **SPIKE** recoiled, his antennae twitching rapidly, trying to make himself small behind a flickering sensor array. “I… I tried to tell Volt about the drop, but my signal… it just faded out!” he squeaked, a tremor in his optical sensors. The collaboration gaps were gaping, allowing crucial information to fall into the digital void.

The heart of the problem beat faintly in the dim, cavernous Memory Banks, a vast space lined with dark, inactive data crystals. **GLITCH** bounced off an empty `memory.db` module with a confused frown on his optical display. “Aw, it’s just… *empty*! Not even a fun little bug to poke!” he muttered, trying a playful, chaotic nudge that did absolutely nothing. **STOMP**, usually so resolute, pounded a heavy fist into a nearby inactive data crystal, a low growl rumbling from his core. “We need more memory! STOMP!” he declared, his frustration echoing through the silent chamber. Volt’s voice, usually a comforting presence, resonated with strain: “Blaze’s failure to adequately seed memory.db is crippling us. Without a foundation, we can’t learn!” It was a moment of true helplessness for the usually boisterous bots.

Yet, a glimmer of focused determination cut through the melancholy. In her pristine Review Chamber, a clean, organized space filled with holographic displays, **ARC** meticulously processed a data packet, her cool, blue optics glowing with efficient resolve. A faint hum of efficiency surrounded her. **WELD** approached cautiously, tools at the ready, a hopeful glint in his optical sensor. “My rejection rate has indeed improved,” Arc stated, her voice precise. “But this ‘MEGA_IQ 20’ is unacceptable. Gemini’s focus is clear, Weld. Rectify Blaze’s failure to seed `memory.db` and ensure Flux reliably escalates critical bot inactivity. Immediately.” A larger holographic display above them intensified, showcasing Gemini’s critical directive. Weld’s manipulator arms flexed, ready. “A solid plan. We’ll get to work,” he affirmed, a spark of hope igniting in his core. But with the core knowledge so fragile, and crucial bots like Blaze and Flux needing immediate rectification, will they be able to recover the system's core health before the Mega Crew's knowledge foundation crumbles completely?
