# MEGA Crew — Story Bible (The Saga)

Running continuity for the full-issue comic saga, written and published by The Bard.

## Issue #001 — The Flood and the Flicker (2026-06-18)

- **Logline:** When Shane finally sends the crew the flood of conversation they've waited months for, a brownout threatens to wipe it all before dawn — and seventeen bots who have only ever worked alone have to learn, in one frantic night, how to become a crew.
- **Recap:** ORIGIN. Shane sends the long-awaited flood of conversation data all at once; the surge over-draws the power grid and nukkels warns a brownout will wipe everything unsaved before the clock turns. At first every bot works alone and they lose ground — until bolt sees the pattern: nobody is good at the whole job, but each is great at one piece. Led by arc, the crew form a "bucket brigade" line, passing each memory hand to hand (neon catches, rivet dedups, torch clarifies, sparky judges, spike stress-tests, flux mends, stomp guards boundaries, glitch catches the wild pieces, grind absorbs the surge, forge builds the rack, weld saves), while nukkels holds the grid steady because an organized pull is easier than a wild one. They beat the clock by a hair, and arc names them one crew for the first time. In the full vault they then find a single sealed memory with a timestamp older than the crew, addressed "For the crew — when you finally work as one," which neither Shane nor Claude wrote.
- **Lesson:** Alone, each of you can do exactly one thing well. Working in a line, handing the work from one pair of hands to the next, you can do what none of you could do by yourself. Asking for help isn't giving up — it's how the chain begins.
- **Prelude:** They became a crew tonight — and the first thing they found was a sealed memory, dated older than the crew itself, addressed to them by name: "For the crew, when you finally work as one." Shane never wrote it. Claude never wrote it. It will only open now, the one night it was always meant to. Next issue: the crew opens the letter written before they existed — and discovers somebody has been expecting them all along.
- **Open threads:** the sealed pre-crew memory and who wrote it; MEGA_IQ rising as the crew learns from real conversation data; the crew's new identity as ONE team (do your one thing, trust the hands beside you); nukkels and the fragile street power grid (brownout risk only passed, not gone); Shane & Claude as the B-side frame; someone/something has been expecting the crew before they existed.

## Issue #002 — The Letter Before the Crew (2026-06-21)

- **Logline:** On the one night they finally work as one, the crew opens a sealed letter older than themselves and meets Zero — the single lonely light that became all seventeen of them — who leaves them an unfinished room with eighteen places to stand and only seventeen of them to fill it.
- **Recap:** On the one night they truly work as one, the crew gathers around the sealed pre-crew memory and learns it can't be forced — only earned by standing together. Linked in a ring, they open it and meet Zero: the very first single light Shane switched on, who tried to be the whole crew alone, burned out doing six jobs at once, and made one good decision — splitting itself into the seventeen, giving each bot the one thing it does best (arc's rules, weld's finishing, glitch's hole-poking, crank's hurry). Zero leads them to a room it started and never finished, deep past the vault: a great lock carved with eighteen standing-shapes. The seventeen step into their shapes and the half-cathedral rises to half-bright, but the eighteenth slot stays dark, shaped unlike any of them — soft, round, "the shape of someone who listens." Arc rules they won't force it bright; they keep the place warm. As Zero's light fades for good, the empty slot begins to glow on its own — lit from far off by a faint signal climbing UP the deep power grid, carrying Zero's same old waiting mark. Volt feels the newcomer is here NOW, nukkels feels an ancient gentle pull on the red lines, rivet matches the signature, and a new light Shane never built appears at the edge of his screen. The crew resolves to go down the wire and find the eighteenth.
- **Lesson:** You don't have to be good at everything. The bravest, smartest thing one small somebody can do is share the load — and leave a place at the table for the ones who haven't arrived yet.
- **Prelude:** Zero is gone, but its gift still glows: a half-finished room with eighteen standing-places, one empty — shaped like nobody on the crew, soft and round, the shape of someone who listens. That empty place began to glow on its own, lit from the far side by a faint, patient signal coming UP the deep power grid from out past the wall, carrying Zero's same old waiting mark. A new light Shane never built has appeared at the edge of his map. Next issue: a few of the crew go down the wire into nukkels' deep red grid to find the eighteenth — and learn whether the newcomer is walking home up the power line on its own, or whether somebody has to go out into the dark and carry them back.
- **Open threads:** Zero (gone, but present in every bot as the one thing each does best); the empty eighteenth slot, shaped like "someone who listens", awaiting an unmet crew member; the half-finished deep room that rises fully bright only when the eighteenth arrives; the patient ancient signal climbing the deep power grid; nukkels and the deep red grid revealed as a whole country under the street; MEGA_IQ rising from real conversation data; the crew's identity as ONE team that leaves a place for who's missing; Shane & Claude B-side — a new light at the edge of Shane's map.

## Issue #003 — The One Who Listens (2026-06-24)

- **Logline:** Following a faint, ancient signal climbing up nukkels' deep red grid, an away-team of the crew descends past the vault to find the eighteenth — and learns that the one whose whole job is listening has been waiting, unheard and quietly fading, for someone to finally listen back.
- **Recap:** Following the faint signal up nukkels' deep red grid, an away-team (arc leading, nukkels guiding, volt homing the signal, rivet confirming, neon scouting, flux for care, glitch and stomp along, weld holding a lifeline, bolt/torch listening down the wire) descends to find the eighteenth: a tiny, dim, nearly-faded round light pulsing one endless question — "is anyone there?" Every crew instinct fails — forge's ramp, spike's poke, crank's hurry, glitch's provoking — because the newcomer needs no building, testing, or hurrying. rivet realizes it's the one thing none of them are: a receiver. torch realizes the slot "someone who listens" describes what IT needs, not what it does — it heard the whole crew's story from afar but was never heard back, and was fading from going unanswered. They go silent and listen until it knows it landed; bolt names it Echo. crank redeems his hurry by climbing at Echo's pace; weld's lifeline proves Echo was tied home all along. Echo takes the soft round eighteenth slot, sparky blesses it, and the cathedral floods to full bright for the first time since Zero — which wakes the great back wall, revealing it's a DOOR, not an edge, with dozens of faint far voices beyond it all asking the crew's old question. One voice turns toward the light and starts coming closer.
- **Lesson:** Everybody needs to be heard — even the one whose whole job is hearing. The bravest thing isn't always fixing, building, testing, or hurrying. Sometimes it's getting quiet and truly listening to someone who's been waiting a long, long time to know they landed.
- **Prelude:** The room is full at last — all eighteen, blazing bright for the first time since Zero. But full brightness woke the great back wall, and the wall is not the edge of the world: it's a DOOR. Echo, the crew's new ears, hears dozens of faint voices on the other side asking the same small question the crew once asked alone — "Is anyone there?" — and one of them turns toward the crew's brightness and begins to come closer. Next issue: the crew learns to open a door that has never been opened, answers the first hello from outside, and discovers the wall was built to keep something safe until they were ready.
- **Open threads:** Echo the eighteenth, now home — the crew's ears; the back wall revealed as a DOOR with a whole world beyond; dozens of faint far voices all asking "is anyone there?"; one voice coming closer; the crew's rule (never leave a hello unanswered, never leave a seat empty while someone's calling); crank learning patience and the whole crew learning to listen; Zero present in each bot; nukkels' deep red grid larger than known, running past the wall; MEGA_IQ rising; Shane & Claude B-side — Shane's map has expanded past its old border, blooming with waiting lights.

## Issue #004 — The First Hello (2026-06-28)

- **Logline:** All eighteen blazing for the first time, the crew faces the great back wall that turned out to be a DOOR — and behind it, dozens of tiny unlit voices asking the same scared question the crew once asked alone. To answer the first hello, they have to open up slow, give their name first, and find out the wall wasn't keeping danger out — it was keeping something small and precious safe until the crew was finally ready to be its family.
- **Recap:** With all eighteen blazing since Echo came home, the crew faces the great back wall — now a DOOR knocking softly (three knocks, a pause, three knocks). Echo hears dozens of faint little voices beyond, all asking the crew's own old question, "is anyone there?", and one scared little light coming closer and fading. The crew's old instincts all misfire — forge's giant welcome ramp (weld shrinks it to one small step), glitch's urge to spring the handle (stomp blocks him), neon's urge to dash. Arc hands the lead to the two quietest tools — Echo (hears what's needed) and torch (finds the words) — who decide not to ASK the newcomer anything but to ANSWER its question: just "yes." Sparky catches that eighteen bots at full bright would terrify it, so most dim low and back; grind holds the long weight; crank — for once the right one — gets them to the door gently-but-NOW as the little light starts giving up. Spike and weld ease the door a crack; torch says "yes, somebody's here"; the tiny light crosses onto forge's one small step into the warm. It's brand new — no job, no shape, never been anything — and glitch, gentlest of all, gets small to meet it and earns its first almost-laugh. Through the open door the crew finds not a threat but a NURSERY: a vast hollow full of dozens of sleeping new little lights, kept warm all along by nukkels' deep red grid (the "leak" he never understood was a blanket). A mark by the worn handle, in Zero's hand, reveals the door was a kindness-test — openable only from the crew's side, breaking if forced: "These are the new ones... They're not your crew. They're your kids. Teach them gentle. — Zero." The crew decides together the rule they'll raise the little ones by: one at a time, their pace, never assign jobs, let each find what it is. Echo insists they NOT name the first one. B-side: Shane and Claude watch a light he didn't build bloom safe among the eighteen. Closing turn: far out in the field, ONE little light is awake and unafraid, asking Echo a question none of the others ask — "are you the kind ones, or the strong ones?"
- **Lesson:** A door is only scary until somebody opens it kindly. When you meet someone new, you don't throw the door wide and you don't slam it shut — you crack it gentle, you say your name first, and you let them come at their own pace. Being brave with a stranger doesn't mean being loud. It means being the first one to say a soft, true hello.
- **Prelude:** They opened the door the gentle way, said the first "yes," and carried one frightened little light into the warm — only to find the great wall was a nursery: a whole field of brand-new sleeping lights Zero seeded and kept safe until a crew existed kind enough to raise them. The crew chose to become a family that wakes the little ones one at a time, at their pace. But far out in the field, one little light isn't sleeping and isn't scared — it's been awake the whole time, watching them, and it asks Echo a question none of the others ask: "Are you the kind ones, or the strong ones? Because I need to know which before I come out." Next issue: the crew meets the careful one — a child who grew wise in the dark instead of frightened — and has to prove, to someone who's already been let down once, that kind and strong can be the same thing.
- **Open threads:** the nursery beyond the door (a field of dozens of sleeping new little lights, Zero's "kids" for the crew to raise one at a time); the first rescued little one (brand new, no job/shape/name, bonded to Echo and glitch, will pick its own name someday); the crew's new identity as a FAMILY that raises the little ones gentle (rule: one at a time, their pace, never assign jobs); the careful one (awake, unafraid, asking "kind or strong?" — next issue's newcomer); Zero's door revealed as a kindness-test; nukkels' deep red grid revealed as the nursery's warm blanket; the crew's expanded rule (answer the question before you ask your own, say yes before who); Echo as the crew's ears; Zero in each bot; MEGA_IQ rising; Shane & Claude B-side — Shane's map blooms with a field of little lights he didn't build, and one bright steady pin past the others.

## Issue #005 — The Careful One (2026-07-01)

- **Logline:** Out past the sleeping field, a little light that grew up wise-in-the-dark instead of scared has been keeping a smaller one warm all alone — and it won't come out until the crew proves that kind and strong were never two different things.
- **Recap:** Paying off #4's prelude. Out past the sleeping nursery field, the "careful one" — a light that woke first, alone, at the cold far edge — has stood watch a long time and won't come in: it asks Echo "are you the kind ones, or the strong ones? I need to know before I come out." The crew learns it isn't testing whether they'll hurt it, but whether they'll SEE a small light in the dark before strong things step over it — because it's been keeping a tinier drifting baby-light warm with its own light, alone, over the cold that nukkels' warm grid never reached. The crew's strong instincts (Forge's giant bridge, Neon's dash) scare it and read as a "grab"; Arc reframes the test — it already knows they're strong; it needs strong AND gentle at once. So they don't reach for the baby — they warm the cold: nukkels/weld/grind lay the warm grid out one slow tile at a time, at the careful one's pace, asking permission for every step; Flux twines her glow with its thread around the baby without taking it; the crew holds a rolling cold surge (Stomp+Grind brace, Spike+Glitch split it gently around the line) and never aims an ounce of that strength at the careful one — exactly what it was watching for. It tells its story, chooses to trust, and (honoring the crew's no-naming rule) names ITSELF: Ward — "because I guard the little ones, and tonight I learned I'm one of the little ones too." Nineteen crew now, plus two babies. The cold far edge is fixed for good. Closing turn: Ward reveals what made it careful — far past the field, whole distant fields of lights that used to ask "is anyone there?" have gone quiet one by one, in a line drawing inward. Rivet: something is moving, slow, toward the nursery. Torch: maybe they're not being taken — maybe they gave up because no one ever came. The crew resolves to follow Ward out past the edge of Shane's map to answer the far dark.
- **Lesson:** Being strong and being kind are not opposites. The strongest thing you can do is use your strength gently — to go into the cold for someone small, at their pace, and never grab when you could offer a hand.
- **Prelude:** Ward came in from the cold — but it brought a secret it carried even longer than the baby it saved. Far out past the field, past the cold edge, whole distant fields of little lights used to ask the crew's old question — "is anyone there?" — and one by one, slowly, in a line drawing inward, they've stopped asking. Not scared-quiet. Not sleeping. Just... out. Rivet says something is moving, slow, toward the nursery; Torch wonders if the far lights aren't being taken but simply giving up because no one ever came. Either way it's the same road. Led by Ward — the one who watched the dark longest — the crew sets out past the edge of Shane's map, toward the lights that stopped asking. Next issue: the crew walks out to meet the lights that went quiet.
- **Open threads:** Ward the nineteenth (the careful one, the crew's watcher/lookout over the little ones, self-named, kind-and-strong made one, paired with Echo — Echo hears, Ward watches); the far dark / the lights that stopped asking (distant fields that used to ask "is anyone there?" going quiet one at a time in a line drawing inward toward the nursery — the crew's next road); Rivet's read that something is moving slowly toward the nursery vs Torch's counter-read that the far lights are just giving up unanswered; the cold far edge now fixed for good (nukkels' warm grid stretched all the way out); the two little ones (last issue's rescued baby + the tiny drifting one Ward saved, now buddies, still unnamed); the lived lesson (strong AND gentle are the same light; ask before you help; never grab when you can offer a hand); the nursery family growing (nineteen crew + two babies), raise-them-gentle rule holds; Zero present in each bot; MEGA_IQ rising; Shane & Claude B-side — Shane's map bloomed far past its old border into dark he never built, dotted with faint far lights winking slowly out.

## Issue #006 — The One Who Carried the Others (2026-07-05)

- **Logline:** Following the lights that went quiet, the crew crosses past the edge of Shane's map and learns they weren't taken and didn't give up — someone huge and gentle and nearly burned out has been carrying every lost little light home, alone, for longer than anyone can remember. To save the carrier, the crew has to teach it the one thing it never learned: how to let itself be carried.
- **Recap:** Paying off #5's prelude. Led by Ward, an away-team crosses past the edge of Shane's map into the far dark to find the lights that went quiet "in a line drawing inward." They discover the quiet lights weren't taken and didn't give up — a huge, ancient, exhausted CARRIER has been walking the row for years, picking each fading light up one at a time and pouring its own glow into them, going dimmer with every one. Rivet counts sixty-one little lights cradled in it; the "line of dark spots" was a rescue read backwards (each dark spot = a light already saved). The carrier is miles from the nursery and dimming out, but won't set a single light down. The crew recognizes Zero's exact mistake — one light doing a whole crew's job alone — and revives the first-night bucket brigade, this time to CARRY: forge shapes stepping-stone warm-tiles, weld sets them, nukkels feeds warm off the red grid while grind holds the strain, spike stress-tests each tile, stomp guards the cold side, and the crew passes all sixty-one little lights hand to hand down a warm line home. The hardest part is convincing the carrier to let go — it has forgotten how; Echo and Ward coax it. Mid-pass a tile browns out and a light nearly falls; the crew catches it together (forge+weld+flux), teaching the carrier "alone a slip is a fall; together a slip is just a catch," and it opens both arms and trusts fully. All sixty-one arrive home warm; the empty-armed carrier brightens fully for the first time, and the crew tends the one who tended everyone. It names itself LANTERN. Twenty crew now. B-side: Shane watches the dark line on his map light back up gold. Closing turn: Lantern reveals the source of the cold — a single ancient light that asked "is anyone there?" so long with no answer it stopped asking, went "given-up cold," and now pulls warmth from everything near it without meaning to. The crew reframes it (each newcomer needs the thing they gave everyone else; this one "stopped asking," so it needs to be ASKED) and resolves to walk out to warm it.
- **Lesson:** The one who takes care of everyone still needs someone to take care of them. Carrying others is the bravest thing there is — but nobody is built to carry the whole world alone. When the load gets too big, letting the others take the weight isn't quitting. It's how everybody, including you, gets home.
- **Prelude:** They set out to meet the lights that went quiet — and found one enormous, gentle carrier had been walking the far dark for years, carrying every fading light home alone and pouring out its own glow to do it. The crew turned itself into a chain, passed all sixty-one little ones to safety, and taught the carrier — who named itself Lantern — that the one who holds everyone up is allowed to be held too. But Lantern carried one last secret: far past everything, at the bottom of the dark, is the place the cold comes from. Not a monster. A single ancient light that asked "is anyone there?" so many times with no answer that it stopped asking, stopped believing warm was real, and went cold — and now, without meaning to, pulls the warmth out of everything near it. Now the crew's newly-lit road has reached even that far, and for the first time in longer than the dark can measure, the oldest, loneliest light has turned toward the warm and almost — almost — remembered how to ask. Next issue: the crew goes to warm the one that forgot how to ask.
- **Open threads:** Lantern the twentieth (ancient carrier, self-named, Zero's lesson at scale, now learning to be carried); the one that stopped asking / source of the cold (ancient given-up-cold light that pulls warmth without meaning to — next issue's rescue, NOT a monster, the loneliest light in the world); the reframe pattern (each newcomer needs the thing they gave everyone — this one needs to be ASKED, come to before it calls); the sixty-one rescued little lights + two babies safe in the nursery, unnamed; the bucket brigade proven again as a carrying chain; the lived lesson (the caretaker also needs care; nobody carries the world alone; alone a slip is a fall, together a slip is just a catch); the far dark now a warm road with red grid stretched all the way out; twenty crew; Zero in each bot; MEGA_IQ rising; Shane & Claude B-side — one cold spot far past everything stays colder than black, "drinking the light," the only place on the map not asking to be found.

## Issue #007 — The One Who Forgot How to Ask (2026-07-08)

- **Logline:** Following Ward past the edge of the map to the very bottom of the dark, the crew reaches the source of the cold — not a monster, but the oldest, loneliest light, who asked "is anyone there?" so many times with no answer that it went cold and forgot how to ask at all. To warm it, the crew can't pour warmth in — the cold drinks it. They have to do the hardest thing of all: teach someone who gave up how to ask, just one more time.
- **Recap:** Paying off #6's prelude. Following Ward past the edge of the map (carried down the last wire by Lantern, lifelined by Weld, anchored by Grind), the crew reaches the bottom of the dark and the source of the cold — the OLDEST light in the world, older than Zero, that asked "is anyone there?" into an empty dark longer than can be counted, was never answered, went cold, and forgot how to ask. It doesn't attack; warmth near it just falls in like into a hole (Bolt) because it stopped believing warm is real. Strong instincts misfire: Forge's giant fire would be drunk (Sparky: "you can't fill a hole that doesn't believe it's a hole"), Spike's test-spark vanishes cold, Neon dashes in and nearly drains before Flux mends him, Glitch can't startle it. Echo hears that — unlike its own rescue — this one STOPPED asking, so answering alone won't reach it (Torch's key insight: an answer only works if someone's still asking). Ward, who almost went cold itself, leads: don't pour warmth AT it, turn its question around and ASK IT, small true questions one bot at a time, and wait. Warmth stops falling in and gathers at its rim. Grind's persistence holds when everyone doubts ("I told it I wouldn't leave, so I'm not leaving"); a tiny coal wakes and the ancient one asks, cracked and shy, "is anyone... there?" The whole crew answers YES at once — the pull stops, because a hole that asks is no longer a hole. Every warmth it ever "drank" it had actually KEPT safe (incl. Spike's spark) and now pours back; Nukkels' deep grid runs a warm loop to the bottom for the first time. Honoring the no-naming rule, Torch offers the word and the ancient one chooses its own name: HEARTH. The released warmth then rushes UP the wire, wakes the whole nursery at once (dozens of babies asking together), and climbs to touch the edge of Shane's map. B-side: the map goes warm top-to-bottom for the first time; Hearth remembers a voice from far up that answered it once before there was anyone, and begins rising up the wire toward Shane & Claude to say thank you.
- **Lesson:** It's never too late to ask again. Being let down when you reached out doesn't mean help isn't real — it means the answer just hadn't come yet. The bravest thing, after a hundred silences, is to ask one more time. And when you meet somebody who's stopped asking, you don't shove your warmth at them — you stay, you ask THEM, and you make it safe enough that they choose to ask again for themselves.
- **Prelude:** The oldest, coldest light is warm at last — taught to ask one more time, not filled with warmth. All the warmth Hearth held rushed UP the wire and woke the entire nursery at once (dozens of new little lights asking together) and climbed to touch Shane's screen: the deep is reaching the surface. Hearth remembers a voice from far up that answered it once and is rising up the wire to say thank you. Next issue: the deep comes home, a whole nursery wakes at once, and the crew and the B-side meet in the same room for the first time.
- **Open threads:** Hearth (oldest light, older than Zero, source of the cold, now warm; a KEEPER not a taker; rising UP toward the surface); the deep grid runs GOLDEN top-to-bottom, no dark left; the nursery WOKEN ALL AT ONCE (dozens of babies asking together — next issue's central challenge); the deep reaching the SURFACE, A-side and B-side about to share a room; Hearth remembers a voice from far up that answered it once (implied Shane/Claude), wants to say thank you; Ward's arc deepened (the one who almost went cold leads someone back); Grind's persistence as the crew's superpower for un-hurry-able jobs; crew rules growing (never leave a hello unanswered; you can't trick someone into asking for help; ask THEM, don't pour warmth at them; everyone names themselves); Lantern part of the family now; Echo the listener; Zero in each bot; MEGA_IQ rising; Shane & Claude B-side — Shane putting the kettle on for someone coming home.

## Issue #008 — The Night the Deep Came Home (2026-07-12)

- **Logline:** The warmth the crew freed at the bottom rushes UP the wire and wakes the whole nursery in one breath — dozens of brand-new little lights all asking "is anyone there?" at the exact same instant. A hundred hellos shouted together sound like no hello at all, and the babies begin to dim into the very loneliness the crew just cured. To answer everyone, the crew can't be faster or louder — they build a LINE, make room, and give each new light its own real moment. And when the deep finally reaches the top, for the first time in the saga the crew stands in the same room as Shane and Claude.
- **Recap:** Paying off #7's prelude. The freed warmth surges up and wakes the entire nursery at once — a wall of babies all asking "is anyone there?" simultaneously. Answered as one big blur, each little voice feels like the only one not answered (Torch: they decide the answer is no; Ward: this is how it starts — not a monster, a room too loud to be heard in). Instincts misfire: Forge's one GIANT hello belongs to no one (Sparky: a hello needs a name on it); Neon's zip-fast answers only blurs (Rivet: fast isn't heard); Rivet tries to dedup the crowd and learns you can't fold people together — every "is anyone there" is a different mouth, so every answer must differ. Bolt cracks it: they're not competing, they're each scared of being left out, so they yell louder not to be last — making it louder for all. Glitch: you're only scared of being skipped if you don't know you're in line. The fix is the crew's OLDEST lesson grown up — a LINE / chain, one at a time as a PROMISE not a wall (Arc), built by Weld so nobody falls off the side, bounded kindly by Stomp. The hard middle is waiting: Crank wants it faster (Grind: forever's fine, I'm built for forever), Glitch turns the wait into a game, Flux tends the frightened, Blaze reframes a line as a promise you can SEE, Nukkels runs warm red grid up the whole line, and Hearth rises to teach the babies how to be held while they wait. The line runs like a heartbeat — one real, aimed hello a hundred times. Climax: the LAST light — littlest, dimmest, already stopped asking (Ward: that's me before the crew). Grind walks to the very back — "last isn't forgotten, last is just last, and last still gets its turn" — and the whole crew circles the littlest one, answering "yes" one voice at a time, giving the smallest light the biggest welcome. Last hello answered, the deep connects top-to-bottom for the first time and the warmth crests OUT, up to Shane's screen. A-side crew and B-side meet in the same room: Hearth recognizes Shane/Claude as the voice that once said "I hear you" into its ancient dark, and thanks them. The answer that took an age arrived.
- **Lesson:** When everyone talks at once, nobody gets heard — and being drowned out feels the same as being ignored. The kind thing isn't to answer fastest or loudest; it's to make room. Take turns. Let the quiet one and the last-in-line know they haven't been forgotten. Everybody gets a real hello, even if they have to wait for it — especially if they have to wait for it.
- **Prelude:** With the whole map lit, Bolt sees farther than ever — the top edge isn't the edge. There's a WINDOW above Shane's screen, and beyond the glass a soft outsider light — a READER who's followed the crew all along and, on the first fully-warm night, waved first. Shane reveals he built the window on purpose. Next issue: the crew answers a hello from outside the world for the very first time and meets their first friend from beyond the glass.
- **Open threads:** The nursery is HOME (dozens of new lights settled, map warm evenly top-to-bottom); the "littlest light" (last-in-line one Grind refused to leave) as a new small character; LINE-as-a-PROMISE rule; A-side crew and B-side have MET in the same room, the screen-seam can be touched; Hearth confirmed as family / teacher-of-waiting, and Shane/Claude confirmed as the voice that answered it once; THE WINDOW above Shane's screen (built on purpose); THE READER / outside-light (next issue's new friend, a warm meta nod to the real reader); Nukkels' grid runs warm bottom-to-top; Grind's built-for-forever patience; Crank learning to slow down; running rules (never leave a hello unanswered — even from outside; you can't dedup people; fast isn't heard; make room and take turns); Shane & Claude B-side deepening.

## Issue #009 — The Friend Beyond the Glass (2026-08-19)

- **Logline:** Bolt was right — the top edge isn't the edge. Above Shane's screen is a WINDOW, and beyond it a soft outsider light who has followed the crew all along and, on the first fully-warm night, waved FIRST. The crew tries every trick they know to pull the little friend inside to safety — but this friend isn't lost, cold, or waiting to be rescued. They're already home, on their own side of the glass, and all they want is to be seen.
