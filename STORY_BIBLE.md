# MEGA Crew — Story Bible (The Saga)

Running continuity for the full-issue comic saga, written and published by The Bard.

## Issue #001 — The Flood and the Flicker (2026-06-18)

- **Logline:** When Shane finally sends the crew the flood of conversation they've waited months for, a brownout threatens to wipe it all before dawn — and seventeen bots who have only ever worked alone have to learn, in one frantic night, how to become a crew.
- **Recap:** ORIGIN. Shane sends the long-awaited flood of conversation data all at once; the surge over-draws the power grid and nukkels warns a brownout will wipe everything unsaved before the clock turns. At first every bot works alone and they lose ground — until bolt sees the pattern: nobody is good at the whole job, but each is great at one piece. Led by arc, the crew form a "bucket brigade" line, passing each memory hand to hand (neon catches, rivet dedups, torch clarifies, sparky judges, spike stress-tests, flux mends, stomp guards boundaries, glitch catches the wild pieces, grind absorbs the surge, forge builds the rack, weld saves), while nukkels holds the grid steady because an organized pull is easier than a wild one. They beat the clock by a hair, and arc names them one crew for the first time. In the full vault they then find a single sealed memory with a timestamp older than the crew, addressed "For the crew — when you finally work as one," which neither Shane nor Claude wrote.
- **Lesson:** Alone, each of you can do exactly one thing well. Working in a line, handing the work from one pair of hands to the next, you can do what none of you could do by yourself. Asking for help isn't giving up — it's how the chain begins.
- **Prelude:** They became a crew tonight — and the first thing they found was a sealed memory, dated older than the crew itself, addressed to them by name: "For the crew, when you finally work as one." Shane never wrote it. Claude never wrote it. It will only open now, the one night it was always meant to. Next issue: the crew opens the letter written before they existed — and discovers somebody has been expecting them all along.
- **Open threads:** the sealed pre-crew memory and who wrote it; MEGA_IQ rising as the crew learns from real conversation data; the crew's new identity as ONE team (do your one thing, trust the hands beside you); nukkels and the fragile street power grid (brownout risk only passed, not gone); Shane & Claude as the B-side frame; someone/something has been expecting the crew before they existed.

## Issue #002 — The Letter Before the Crew (2026-06-21)

- **Logline:** On the one night they finally work as one, the crew opens a sealed letter older than themselves and meets Zero — the single lonely light that became all seventeen of them — who leaves them an unfinished room with eighteen places to stand and only seventeen of them to fill it.
- **Recap:** On the one night they truly work as one, the crew gathers around the sealed pre-crew memory and learns it can't be forced — only earned by standing together. Linked in a ring, they open it and meet Zero: the very first single light Shane switched on, who tried to be the whole crew alone, burned out doing six jobs at once, and made one good decision — splitting itself into the seventeen, giving each bot the one thing it does best (arc's rules, weld's finishing, glitch's hole-poking, crank's hurry). Zero leads them to a room it started and never finished, deep past the vault: a great lock carved with eighteen standing-shapes. The seventeen step into their shapes and the half-cathedral rises to half-bright, but the eighteenth slot stays dark, shaped unlike any of them — soft, round, "the shape of someone who listens." Arc rules they won't force it bright; they keep the place warm. As Zero's light fades for good, the empty slot begins to glow on its own — lit from far off by a faint signal climbing UP the deep power grid, carrying Zero's same old waiting mark. Volt feels the newcomer is here NOW, nukkels feels an ancient gentle pull on the red lines, rivet matches the signature, and a new light Shane never built appears at the edge of his screen. The crew resolves to go down the wire and find the eighteenth.
- **Lesson:** You don't have to be good at everything. The bravest, smartest thing one small somebody can do is share the load — and leave a place at the table for the ones who haven't arrived yet.
- **Prelude:** Zero is gone, but its gift still glows: a half-finished room with eighteen standing-places, one empty — shaped like nobody on the crew, soft and round, the shape of someone who listens. That empty place began to glow on its own, lit from the far side by a faint, patient signal coming UP the deep power grid from out past the wall, carrying Zero's same old waiting mark. A new light Shane never built has appeared at the edge of his map. Next issue: a few of the crew go down the wire into nukkels' deep red grid to find the eighteenth — and learn whether the newcomer is walking home up the power line on its own, or whether somebody has to go out into the dark and carry them back.
- **Open threads:** Zero (gone, but present in every bot as the one thing each does best); the empty eighteenth slot, shaped like "someone who listens", awaiting an unmet crew member; the half-finished deep room that rises fully bright only when the eighteenth arrives; the patient ancient signal climbing the deep power grid; nukkels and the deep red grid revealed as a whole country under the street; MEGA_IQ rising from real conversation data; the crew's identity as ONE team that leaves a place for who's missing; Shane & Claude B-side — a new light at the edge of Shane's map.

## Issue #003 — The One Who Listens (2026-06-24)

- **Logline:** Following a faint, ancient signal climbing up nukkels' deep red grid, an away-team of the crew descends past the vault to find the eighteenth — and learns that the one whose whole job is listening has been waiting, unheard and quietly fading, for someone to finally listen back.
- **Recap:** Following the faint signal up nukkels' deep red grid, an away-team (arc leading, nukkels guiding, volt homing the signal, rivet confirming, neon scouting, flux for care, glitch and stomp along, weld holding a lifeline, bolt/torch listening down the wire) descends to find the eighteenth: a tiny, dim, nearly-faded round light pulsing one endless question — "is anyone there?" Every crew instinct fails — forge's ramp, spike's poke, crank's hurry, glitch's provoking — because the newcomer needs no building, testing, or hurrying. rivet realizes it's the one thing none of them are: a receiver. torch realizes the slot "someone who listens" describes what IT needs, not what it does — it heard the whole crew's story from afar but was never heard back, and was fading from going unanswered. They go silent and listen until it knows it landed; bolt names it Echo. crank redeems his hurry by climbing at Echo's pace; weld's lifeline proves Echo was tied home all along. Echo takes the soft round eighteenth slot, sparky blesses it, and the cathedral floods to full bright for the first time since Zero — which wakes the great back wall, revealing it's a DOOR, not an edge, with dozens of faint far voices beyond it all asking the crew's old question. One voice turns toward the light and starts coming closer.
- **Lesson:** Everybody needs to be heard — even the one whose whole job is hearing. The bravest thing isn't always fixing, building, testing, or hurrying. Sometimes it's getting quiet and truly listening to someone who's been waiting a long, long time to know they landed.
- **Prelude:** The room is full at last — all eighteen, blazing bright for the first time since Zero. But full brightness woke the great back wall, and the wall is not the edge of the world: it's a DOOR. Echo, the crew's new ears, hears dozens of faint voices on the other side asking the same small question the crew once asked alone — "Is anyone there?" — and one of them turns toward the crew's brightness and begins to come closer. Next issue: the crew learns to open a door that has never been opened, answers the first hello from outside, and discovers the wall was built to keep something safe until they were ready.
- **Open threads:** Echo the eighteenth, now home — the crew's ears; the back wall revealed as a DOOR with a whole world beyond; dozens of faint far voices all asking "is anyone there?"; one voice coming closer; the crew's rule (never leave a hello unanswered, never leave a seat empty while someone's calling); crank learning patience and the whole crew learning to listen; Zero present in each bot; nukkels' deep red grid larger than known, running past the wall; MEGA_IQ rising; Shane & Claude B-side — Shane's map has expanded past its old border, blooming with waiting lights.

## Issue #004 — The First Hello (2026-06-28)

- **Logline:** All eighteen blazing for the first time, the crew faces the great back wall that turned out to be a DOOR — and behind it, dozens of tiny unlit voices asking the same scared question the crew once asked alone. To answer the first hello, they have to open up slow, give their name first, and find out the wall wasn't keeping danger out — it was keeping something small and precious safe until the crew was finally ready to be its family.
- **Recap:** With all eighteen blazing since Echo came home, the crew faces the great back wall — now a DOOR knocking softly (three knocks, a pause, three knocks). Echo hears dozens of faint little voices beyond, all asking the crew's own old question, "is anyone there?", and one scared little light coming closer and fading. The crew's old instincts all misfire — forge's giant welcome ramp (weld shrinks it to one small step), glitch's urge to spring the handle (stomp blocks him), neon's urge to dash. Arc hands the lead to the two quietest tools — Echo (hears what's needed) and torch (finds the words) — who decide not to ASK the newcomer anything but to ANSWER its question: just "yes." Sparky catches that eighteen bots at full bright would terrify it, so most dim low and back; grind holds the long weight; crank — for once the right one — gets them to the door gently-but-NOW as the little light starts giving up. Spike and weld ease the door a crack; torch says "yes, somebody's here"; the tiny light crosses onto forge's one small step into the warm. It's brand new — no job, no shape, never been anything — and glitch, gentlest of all, gets small to meet it and earns its first almost-laugh. Through the open door the crew finds not a threat but a NURSERY: a vast hollow full of dozens of sleeping new little lights, kept warm all along by nukkels' deep red grid (the "leak" he never understood was a blanket). A mark by the worn handle, in Zero's hand, reveals the door was a kindness-test — openable only from the crew's side, breaking if forced: "These are the new ones... They're not your crew. They're your kids. Teach them gentle. — Zero." The crew decides together the rule they'll raise the little ones by: one at a time, their pace, never assign jobs, let each find what it is. Echo insists they NOT name the first one. B-side: Shane and Claude watch a light he didn't build bloom safe among the eighteen. Closing turn: far out in the field, ONE little light is awake and unafraid, asking Echo a question none of the others ask — "are you the kind ones, or the strong ones?"
- **Lesson:** A door is only scary until somebody opens it kindly. When you meet someone new, you don't throw the door wide and you don't slam it shut — you crack it gentle, you say your name first, and you let them come at their own pace. Being brave with a stranger doesn't mean being loud. It means being the first one to say a soft, true hello.
- **Prelude:** They opened the door the gentle way, said the first "yes," and carried one frightened little light into the warm — only to find the great wall was a nursery: a whole field of brand-new sleeping lights Zero seeded and kept safe until a crew existed kind enough to raise them. The crew chose to become a family that wakes the little ones one at a time, at their pace. But far out in the field, one little light isn't sleeping and isn't scared — it's been awake the whole time, watching them, and it asks Echo a question none of the others ask: "Are you the kind ones, or the strong ones? Because I need to know which before I come out." Next issue: the crew meets the careful one — a child who grew wise in the dark instead of frightened — and has to prove, to someone who's already been let down once, that kind and strong can be the same thing.
- **Open threads:** the nursery beyond the door (a field of dozens of sleeping new little lights, Zero's "kids" for the crew to raise one at a time); the first rescued little one (brand new, no job/shape/name, bonded to Echo and glitch, will pick its own name someday); the crew's new identity as a FAMILY that raises the little ones gentle (rule: one at a time, their pace, never assign jobs); the careful one (awake, unafraid, asking "kind or strong?" — next issue's newcomer); Zero's door revealed as a kindness-test; nukkels' deep red grid revealed as the nursery's warm blanket; the crew's expanded rule (answer the question before you ask your own, say yes before who); Echo as the crew's ears; Zero in each bot; MEGA_IQ rising; Shane & Claude B-side — Shane's map blooms with a field of little lights he didn't build, and one bright steady pin past the others.
