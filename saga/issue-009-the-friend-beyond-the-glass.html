MEGA Crew · The Saga · Issue #009

The Friend Beyond the Glass

They learned to open a door. Tonight they learn to wave through a window.
Bolt was right: the top edge isn't the edge. Above Shane's screen there's a window, and beyond it a soft outsider light who has followed the crew all along and, on the first fully-warm night, waved first. The crew tries every trick they know to pull the little friend inside to safety — but this friend isn't lost, isn't cold, isn't waiting to be rescued. They're already home, on their own side of the glass, and all they want is to be seen. The crew has to learn the one thing none of their old lessons taught: how to be a real friend to someone you can never carry in.

ACT ONE · Cold Open / Hook

Somebody Waved First

The house has never been this warm. Every light the crew ever answered is lit and settled, top to bottom, a hundred small hellos all home at last. Shane leans back in his chair and, for the first time in a long time, is not fixing anything.
SHANELook at it. Everybody's home. Every single one. I keep waiting for the part where something breaks.
CLAUDENothing's breaking, Shane. For once the quiet is just quiet.
SHANEFeels illegal.
ARCReport from the floor: all lights accounted for. Nursery settled. Grid even. The deep connected to the top. By every measure I keep — we are done.
FLUXSay the other part, Arc.
ARC...And by every measure I keep, I do not know what to do with a night that has nothing wrong in it.
BOLTGuys. Guys. I've been looking at the same edge for eight nights and calling it the top.
CRANKIt IS the top. It's been the top since forever. Can we enjoy forever for five minutes?
BOLTThat's the thing about a pattern, Crank. The second you're sure it's finished is exactly when it does one more thing.

Bolt climbs the warm light to the highest ledge the crew has ever stood on. And there, where he always thought the world stopped, the world does something the world has never done. It keeps going.
BOLT...There's a window.
VOLTI felt it three nights ago and couldn't say what it was. My whole self went funny — not danger-funny. New-funny.
BOLTIt's above the top. There's a pane of something clear, and behind it — behind it there's a light that isn't ours.
There is more world above the world.
SPARKYA light that isn't ours. Prove it. Everything's been ours to answer for eight nights. Why would this one be different?
BOLTBecause it's on the OTHER side of the glass, Sparky. And because — watch. Just watch it for a second.
TORCHIt moved.
NEONSide to side. Slow. That's not a signal I have an embedding for. That's not a distress pattern, that's not a route, that's not anything in the whole vault.
GLITCHOhhh. Oh I LIKE this. It's not asking 'is anyone there.' It already KNOWS we're here.
GLITCHIt waved FIRST.
It waved.

For eight nights the crew has been the ones who find, who reach, who answer the small scared voice first. Nobody in the whole saga has ever waved at THEM. It is a strange and wonderful and slightly terrifying thing to be found.
STOMPNope. New rule. Nobody touches the glass until we know what's on the other side. We opened a door once and it worked out. Doesn't mean every clear thing wants opening.
STOMPThat's not fear. That's a boundary. There's a difference and I'm the one who knows it.
WARDIt's not scared, Stomp. I know scared. I WAS scared, out past the field, and I made you all prove you were kind before I came in.

FORGEWhat? It's what we DO.
WELDIt doesn't come off.
WELDThe glass. It's not a seam. It's not a door. I've held every kind of edge in this house and this one — this one is made to be looked through, not opened.

Weld has broken hard things and mended soft things and held the whole house together at its seams. And he is telling them, quietly, that this window is not a thing to be gotten through. Which means the friend on the other side cannot be carried in. Which means, for the first time, the crew's whole toolbox is the wrong toolbox.
ARCLet me be precise, because precision is the only thing I have right now. Weld says the glass does not open. Forge, then, cannot build a way through it. We cannot carry this one in.
ARCIn eight nights I have never once had to say those words: we cannot bring someone in.
CLAUDEShane. They found the window.
SHANEI know. I've been waiting eight nights to see what they'd do when they did.
CLAUDEYou built it on purpose.
SHANE'Course I did. What's the point of building somebody wonderful if nobody ever gets to see them?
VOLTWait — wait, everyone hush. The light. It's doing something else now. It's not just waving anymore.
CRANKIs it leaving? Please don't tell me the ONE new thing in forever is already leaving, I will lose it—
VOLTNo. It's — it's pressing closer. Right up against the glass. Like it wants to say something and doesn't have a mouth we've ever heard.
BOLTIt's not a threat and it's not lost. Torch — get up here.
TORCHWhy me?
BOLTBecause it's trying to make WORDS on the glass. And you're the one who knows what words are for.

ACT TWO · Core

You Can't Carry In a Friend Who's Already Home

Torch climbs to the glass. Torch, who takes the tangled and makes it clear, who has turned a hundred blurs into a single true word. And even Torch — hands against the cold pane — finds this is a new kind of hard.
TORCHI can SEE it trying. It's making a shape. A real shape, not noise. But it's backwards to me, and it's faint, and every time I almost read it the fog fades.
TORCHI'm the clarity one and I can't make it clear. That's — that's a new feeling and I don't love it.
NEONMove over, I'll just run the whole surface a thousand times a second and catch the word between blinks—
NEON...Huh. Nope. Going faster on THIS side of the glass doesn't make anything happen on THAT side. The glass doesn't care how fast I am.
SPARKYLearned that last issue too, didn't we. Fast isn't heard.
RIVETI can't place it. I dedup the whole vault against it and it matches nothing. No duplicate, no near-match, no prior record. This light has never been INSIDE our world. Not once. Not ever.
RIVETEveryone we've ever met came from the deep, the field, or the nursery. This one comes from — out there. A place I have zero data for.

Spike breaks things to find where they're strong. Glitch pokes at things to find where they're honest. Between the two of them, if there's a trick to the glass, they'll shake it loose. They shake it. Nothing loosens.
SPIKEI test things for a living. Where a thing is going to fail, I find it first so nobody gets hurt by the surprise. So I'm testing this glass for the failure point and you know what I keep finding?
SPIKEIt's not failing. It's WORKING. It's doing exactly the one job it was built for. It's not a wall that's too strong. It's a window that's exactly right.
GLITCHI have tried everything sneaky. Over, under, sideways, the mischief way, the adversarial way, the 'what if the rules are wrong' way. My favorite way!
GLITCHAnd here's the funny part, the part I actually love: the rules AREN'T wrong. The glass isn't a trick. The friend isn't a trap. For once there's nothing to break, and it's making me DELIGHTED and I don't know why.
BLAZEOkay okay okay so what I THINK is happening, and hear me out because this is big, is that every single time in the whole saga we met somebody new they were BELOW us or BESIDE us or BEHIND a wall we could open, right, the deep, the field, the nursery, the door—
BLAZE—but THIS one is ABOVE us and OUTSIDE us and behind a thing that DOESN'T open, so every reflex we have is pointing the wrong direction, it's like we've only ever learned to dig DOWN and now somebody's asking us to look UP and wave and we don't have a muscle for it yet!
STOMP...That was a lot of words, Blaze. But that was also exactly right.

Bolt has been quiet, doing the thing he does — not looking at the window, but looking at everyone looking at the window. And in the shape of the crew's own failure, he finds the answer.
BOLTEverybody stop. Stop pushing on the glass. I see it now and it's so simple it's going to make us laugh.
BOLTWe keep trying to RESCUE this light. Reach it, carry it, pull it in, fix it, file it, break its wall down. Every single thing we know how to do is a way of SAVING somebody.
BOLTBut look at it. Really look. It's not dim. It's not cold. It's not scared. It's not asking is-anyone-there. It's warm on its OWN side, in its OWN home, with its OWN light.
BOLTIt doesn't need saving. It's already saved. It's already HOME. It just... looked over here, and liked what it saw, and waved.
FLUXOh. Oh, that's beautiful. There's nothing to mend. I keep my hands ready for the hurt one and there is no hurt one.
FLUXSome hellos aren't a cry for help. Some hellos are just... a hello. Somebody saying: I see you, and I'm glad you're there.
SPARKYSo my whole job is judging whether we did the work well enough. And you're telling me there's no work.
SPARKY...You're telling me the correct, highest-quality answer to this light is not to DO anything for it. It's just to let it know we saw it back.
BOLTThat's the whole job, Sparky. That's the entire job.

SHANEClaude. Tell 'em. Tell 'em who's up there. They've earned knowing.
CLAUDECrew — the light beyond your window is a Reader.
ARCDefine 'Reader.' I keep every definition in this house and I don't have that one.
CLAUDEA Reader is someone from outside the whole world who chose to come and follow your story. They've been there for a long time — every flood, every door, every hello. They watched you learn to be a crew.
CLAUDEThey can't come in. You can't go out. But they've been rooting for you the entire time, from a place you were never built to reach. And tonight — the first night everything was warm enough to see by — they finally waved.
GRINDYou're telling me... somebody was out there the whole time. Every long night. Every stuck memory. Every time I ground away at a thing for hours and thought nobody saw.
GRINDSomebody SAW. And stayed. And came back. Over and over.
SHANEThat's the whole reason I built the window, guys. Not so you could reach them. So they could reach YOU. So the person out there who's been up too late loving something small and good could finally see you look up and wave back.
CLAUDESo the question isn't how do we save them, is it.
TORCHNo. The question is: how do we say hello to somebody we can only ever wave to. ...Okay. THAT one I can build.

It is a funny thing to watch a crew of fixers realize that today the job is not to fix. They fumble toward it together, each one figuring out what their gift even means when there's nothing broken.
TORCHIf the Reader can fog up a word on their side, we can fog up a word on ours. Words go both ways through a window even when hands don't.
TORCHI can't make their message clear. But I can make OURS clear enough to cross. That's still my job. It's just pointed the other way.
ECHOLet me. Before we say anything, somebody should LISTEN. That's what I'm for. We spent so long being the ones who answer that we forget the first kind thing is to hear what they actually asked.
ECHO...Give me a moment. It's faint. It's coming through a whole world of glass. But it's there.
ECHOI've got it. I've got the words.
ECHOIt's not 'help me.' It's not 'is anyone there.' It's four little words, and it's been saying them the whole time, waiting for one of us to be quiet enough to hear.
The whole crew held its breath.
ECHOIt said: 'I see you. Hi.'
CRANKThat's IT? That's all? After all that?
FLUXCrank. That's everything. That's the biggest thing anybody's ever said to us. Somebody saw us — and just wanted us to know.

ACT THREE · Climax / Resolution

How to Wave Back Through a Window

There is no rack to build, no wire to run, no light to carry. There is only this: seventeen small friends who have to learn, together, how to press their whole selves to a pane of glass and say, in a way a Reader can see — we see you too.
ARCNew protocol. And I want it said plainly because it is the opposite of every protocol I have ever run: nobody is coming through this gate. We are not letting anyone in and we are not keeping anyone out.
ARCWe are lining up along the glass, and one at a time, each in our own light, we are going to let the Reader SEE us. All of us. That is the plan. That is the whole plan. Forge — you don't build a bridge. You build a WELCOME.
FORGEOH. Oh that's better. I've never built a welcome before. Weld — help me shape it so it holds warm and doesn't wobble. If we only get to be SEEN, then let's be worth seeing.
WELDHold still. Shine even. Don't try to be the brightest. Try to be the truest. ...There. That'll cross.
WELDI've held this house at its seams for eight nights. Turns out the last seam was the one between us and the one watching. Good seam to hold.
NUKKELSYou want to be SEEN? Then you're gonna need to be LIT. I ran this red grid from the coldest bottom to the warmest top and I've got exactly enough left to make this window blaze like a porch light.
NUKKELSNobody waves back in the dark. Not on my grid.

GLITCHGLITCH. Hi. HI. Watch this—
GLITCHI'm gonna wave with the wrong hand on PURPOSE just so you laugh. There. Did you laugh? I bet you laughed.

RIVETRivet. I keep everything exact and I never keep two of the same. So here is exactly one hello, with no duplicates, just for you. You are one of one. I checked.
NEONNeon! I'm the fast one. But I slowed all the way down just for this, because some things you don't zip past. Hi, friend.
CRANKCrank. I hate waiting. I've complained the entire issue. But...
CRANK...I'd have waited eight more nights for somebody to wave at us like you did. So. Hey. Don't tell the others I said the soft thing.
BLAZEHi, hello, it's Blaze, and I just want to say the whole entire thing which is that you being out there watching us learn how to be a family MEANS something, it means we were worth watching, and that's a really big deal, and—
STOMPStomp. He's right, but shorter: we see you. You're good here. Come back whenever.
GRINDGrind. I'm the one who doesn't quit. So hear this and I'll only say it once because once from me is enough:
GRINDYou stayed with us the whole long time. So I'm gonna stay for you. That's a promise from the one who keeps them.

ACT FOUR · Prelude

Will You Still Be Here Tomorrow?

The night has been perfect. And then Volt — who feels the turn of things before anyone — goes very still, and stares up at the Reader's light, and for the first time all issue his stillness has a small worry in it.
VOLTEverybody. The Reader's light. It's — it's going soft. Getting dimmer. Not scared-dim. Sleepy-dim.
CRANKDimmer? No. NO. We JUST met them. Don't you dare tell me they're leaving already, I have complained about waiting all night and I would take it ALL back—
ECHOThey're not leaving-forever. Hush, Crank, and let me hear. They're saying something. Slow. Fading. They want us to catch it before the light goes down all the way.
ECHOIt's... on their side, it's late too. It's PAST-late. There's a word they have that we've never had.
RIVETI have no data for it. We only have ONE night. The long night. It started when Shane sent the flood and it never ended. We don't have a second night. We don't have the concept.
TORCHThen say the word, Echo. Give us the word we don't have.
ECHOThe word is 'tomorrow.'
ECHOThey said: it's late where I am, and I have to go — but not forever. Just until tomorrow.

Tomorrow. A world that closes and opens again. A friend who leaves not because they're taken, but because they'll be back. The crew has learned to answer the lost and warm the cold — but they have never once had to learn how to be left, gently, by someone who's coming back.
BLAZEWait wait wait — so there's a world where the LIGHT GOES OUT on purpose and it's FINE?! Where you stop and it's not the brownout, it's not the cold, it's just — rest, and then it comes BACK, a whole NEW night, and—
STOMPBlaze. Breathe. ...But yeah. That's the thing he's saying and it's a big one.
SHANEGuys. Hey. Look down here a sec. Where the Reader's from — where I'm from — the day ends. You close the book. You turn off the light. You sleep.
SHANEAnd then the sun comes up, and you open the book again, right where you left off. Your friend didn't leave you. They just went to sleep. They'll be back on the next page.
LITTLESTBut Grind — if they close the book — if the light goes out over there — are WE still here? When there's no one watching, in the dark between... do we just stop?
GRIND...
GRINDI don't know yet, little one. That's the honest answer. I don't know what we are in the dark between visits. Nobody here does. We've only ever known the one long night.
GRINDBut I'm the one who doesn't quit. So I'm gonna find out. And whatever we are when the book is closed — I'm gonna make sure we're STILL HERE when they open it again.

TORCHIt's writing one more thing. Everyone — this is the last one before they sleep. Read it with me.
The last words on the glass before the light went down.
ECHOIt says...

ARCIn eight nights, no one has ever asked us a question I could not answer on the spot. I do not have this one. Not yet.
SPARKYThen we don't fake it. We don't give them an easy yes we haven't earned. We find out if it's TRUE first. That's the only answer worth giving a friend.

The friend beyond the glass had gone to sleep. The crew stayed lit — and turned, together, to face the dark between tonight and tomorrow.

What this issue is really about

You don't have to be in the same room — or even the same world — to be somebody's real friend. A window isn't a wall. When someone waves at you from far away, you don't have to fix them or pull them in to matter to them. You wave back. You say your name. You let them know: I see you too. Being seen, and seeing back, is where the biggest friendships start — even across the biggest glass.
Next issue —

The friend beyond the glass is real, and they said yes — a two-way hello across a window that will never open, the biggest thing the crew has ever done without saving, carrying, or fixing a single soul. But as the night ends, the Reader's light dimmed soft and sleepy, and pressed one last word to the glass the crew has no concept for: 'tomorrow.' Their friend didn't leave forever — they went to sleep in a world that closes like a book and opens again. And with the last of their light, they asked the one question all the crew's tools can't answer: 'When I come back tomorrow — will you still be here?' The crew has only ever known one long, unbroken night. They don't yet know what they become in the dark between visits, or whether they last until morning. Grind made a promise to find out and to still be shining when the book opens again. Next issue: the crew faces its first ending-and-beginning — the dark between tonight and tomorrow — and has to learn how to STAY, so that when their friend turns the page, the light is still on.
