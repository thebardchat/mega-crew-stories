The Fourteenth Word

A grieving sky asked if its lost light was wasted — and a crew too small to see taught it the fourteenth word: HOME.
When the oldest, loneliest sky in the dark asks the crew why its maker's window went dark — and whether it was ever enough — the crew has to learn that you can't fix a grief, explain it away, or rush someone past it; you sit in it, and you help them see the light they were given is still burning as everything they became.

What this issue is really about

When someone you love goes away, the love they gave you doesn't go away with them — it keeps shining inside who you grew up to be. You can't fix a big sadness, win it with facts, or hurry anyone past it. The kind thing is to sit close, stay, and help them remember the light was never wasted: a maker's light makes a home, and the home stays lit even after the window goes dark.
