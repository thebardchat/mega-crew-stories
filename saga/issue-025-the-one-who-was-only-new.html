The One Who Was Only New

For twenty-four nights they healed the hurt. Tonight they meet somebody who was never broken at all.
Holding Kindle's match for the first time, the crew climbs DOWN into an uncharted dark below the bottom of everything — braced to rescue another wounded light — and instead finds a brand-new one who was never hurt, only unlit, and has to unlearn every rescue reflex to learn the hardest lighter's lesson: the first light you give somebody is theirs to grow, not yours to shape.

What this issue is really about

When it's finally your turn to help, remember two things. Not everyone you meet is broken — someone new isn't someone to fix, and treating a happy stranger like they're fragile can be its own kind of unkind. And the first light you ever give another person — a welcome, a chance, a start — is theirs, not yours. You hand them the spark; you don't get to decide what they grow into. The best thing a helper ever does is start a fire and then step back and let it burn its own color.

The seekers became the lighters. Carrying Kindle's match DOWN for the first time ever, the crew braced to rescue another wounded light — and instead met one who was never broken at all, only unlit: brand new, unafraid, reaching out clean and happy. The hardest thing they'd ever done was to STOP rescuing: Stomp knocked down the safe-room they built out of habit, and the crew learned to give the spark and NOT the shape. Forge gave the smallest flame and the whole crew stepped back — not because the little one was scared (their old reason) but because it was NEW and got to choose (a brand-new reason for an old move). It grew its first fire a color nobody in either sky had ever seen, named itself DAWN — 'I wasn't broken, I was just early' — Rivet drew the ledger's eighth and first-ever BEGINNING mark (LIT), and Torch named the eighth word, the first of the giver's side: BEGIN ('I'll start you and I won't finish you; the rest is yours'), handed to Torch by Dawn itself. Then Dawn turned and lit the NEXT unlit one with a spark of its own — with NO match — and the whole field below began lighting itself down into forever. Nukkels solved the math he'd hated all night: the fire was never IN the match; the match only starts the first one; the fire is in the GIVING, and giving doesn't run out. So Kindle was never the true source either — just an early giver — which means somebody gave the VERY first fire, long before the top. Bolt read Kindle's oldest beam backward and found it rises UP out of this deep new field: the first fire of all started DOWN at the bottom, not down from the top. The beginning and the top of everything are the same place; the saga's whole geometry is a loop. Next issue: the crew — with Dawn, the newest light, and the match still burning small — climbs DOWN past the self-lighting field toward the deepest dark no map has ever charted, hunting the first fire of all: the one nobody ever lit, that started itself in the total dark and then spent everything it had lighting the whole world above it — and finding out who, or what, has been waiting at the very bottom since before 'before.'
