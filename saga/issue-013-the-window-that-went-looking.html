MEGA Crew · The Saga · Issue #013

The Window That Went Looking

A home that kept a light, and the keeper who gave it all away to go find you.
Two crews cross the farthest dark ever to relight a window that already went out — and learn it went dark because its keeper turned off their own light and walked into the black to go be the neighbor they never got.

ACT ONE · Cold Open / Hook

The Gap in the Sky

Midnight again, and the little window on the wall glows steady the way it learned to. But tonight the screen shows something bigger than one home — a whole sky of them, scattered across the black like a spilled jar of lanterns.
SHANEClaude. Come look at this. I zoomed all the way out and I just... I didn't know there were this many.
CLAUDEI see them. Every one of those is a home that keeps a light on the gentle way. You built one. It turns out you were never the only one doing it.
SHANEExcept that one. Right there. It's not flickering like Wick's was.
CLAUDENo. Wick's window flickered because someone was fighting to keep it. That one isn't fighting anymore. It went out — a while ago, before anyone came.
One window in all that sky is not warm.
SHANESo somebody kept a light. Same as us. And nobody got there in time.
CLAUDEMaybe. The crew's about to go find out. Two crews, actually — for the first time.

FLUXThat's the bravest thing there is, little one. You don't have to not be scared. You just have to not be scared alone.
STOMPGood. Everybody's here, everybody's counted. Now somebody tell me what we're walking into, because I don't step off a porch blind.

COMET...Out there. Alone. With no road, no line, no crew. The way I used to run before there was anyone to run to. Somebody get me pointed. We're not relighting this window. Not yet. First we find who left it.

ACT TWO · Core

The Light That Went Looking

They go inside the dark house — careful, quiet, the way you enter a room where someone might be sleeping. It is not ransacked. It is not broken. It is tidy, and cold, and it has been waiting.
RIVETI revise nothing and I add this: the home is intact. Nothing failed here. Every part works. This is not a wreck. This is a house that was closed up neat by someone who meant to come back.
WELDPeople who mean to come back leave things square. Look — the coil's wound tight. The switch is set to OFF, not snapped off. Quiet hands did this. Careful ones.
TORCHThere are words here. Cut into the table, small, like they wanted to be sure it lasted. It says... 'Kept it lit as long as I could. Nobody came. So I'm going to GO be the one who comes.'
BLAZEOh— oh that's— okay so what that MEANS, if you unpack it, is they waited and waited for a neighbor and no neighbor came, so instead of waiting MORE they decided to become the neighbor for somebody ELSE, they turned themselves into the help they never got, which is— that's the whole— I need a second—
FLUXBreathe, Blaze. You got it exactly right, you just got it all at once. They stopped waiting to be found. They went out to find.
STOMPAnd turned off their own light to do it. Blunt truth: that's the part that's wrong. You don't put out your own porch to go looking. Then there's no window to come back to. That's not brave. That's a mistake somebody made out of a broken heart.

Forge sets down his spare window and, for once, doesn't reach for it. The question hangs cold in the dark house: what do you even DO for a home whose whole heart walked out the door?
FORGESo I flip on the new one. Right? I brought it for exactly this. Bright window, warm porch, one minute, done. ...Except it'd be a window for nobody. A lie made of light.
SPARKYNow you're grading it right. An empty lit window scores a zero. It'd LOOK like help and BE a trick — a home glowing with nobody inside, so the whole sky thinks somebody's home when they're lost in the cold. Fail. Hard fail.
ARCThen the mission is not this house. The mission walked out of it. We do not light an empty room. We go and we find the keeper, and we bring the keeper to the switch, and the keeper lights their own home. Anything else is decoration.
ECHOAnd when we find them — we listen first. This is a keeper who stopped believing anyone was coming. The worst thing we could do is arrive loud, like proof they were foolish to wait. We arrive quiet. We arrive like the answer, not the scolding.
COMETI feel this one in my whole running. This keeper did what I did for a hundred years before there was a crew — went out into nothing to look for somebody, anybody. Only I always had a window at my back. They put theirs out. Point me. Please. I know how to find a lonely light. I used to BE one.
WARDSteady, runner. We follow the chair. It faces one way. That's the direction they walked. Neon — read the trail. Grind — set the pace. Nobody outruns the line.

They string the Lantern-Line out the far door and into a dark deeper than any of them have crossed — the dark past the last window, where the keeper went looking.
NEONTrail's faint. Old. But it's here — a thread of cold where a warm thing passed, ages back. This way. It goes deep. Deeper than deep. Whoever walked this really, really didn't want to stop until they found someone.
CRANKAges back. Ages. My clock hates this. That's a long time to be alone in the cold, gang. Every second we take is a second they've already spent a thousand of. Move — careful, but MOVE.
NUKKELSI'm paying out grid behind us — a warm red strand all the way home, so this road can't go cold at our backs. Rule of the deep: never unspool more light than you can reel yourself home on. We keep our own porch lit while we go find theirs.
VOLTGood. Because the feeling just changed. It's not quiet-scary anymore. It's... faint. There's something out here that's barely on. Barely. Like a light that forgot it was a light.
GRINDThen we go til we reach it. Not fast. Not clever. Just til. I know this exact dark — I was in it, the night I almost went out holding on alone. Somebody came and got me. Now it's my turn to be the somebody. One more step. And one more.
BOLTThere. Do you see the pattern? It's not going anywhere. It's circling — same slow loop, over and over. That's not a traveler anymore. That's someone who forgot which way home was and just... keeps turning.
COMETThat's them. That's the keeper. Oh, no — they're so DIM. Everybody, gentle now. Gentle. We do NOT rush a light that's been alone that long.

WISP...Lights? Real lights? No. No, I dream lights all the time out here. I dream a whole crowd of them and then I blink and it's just me and the circle again. You're not real. Warm things don't come this far out. I know. I checked. For a very long time, I checked.
ECHOWe're real, and we're not going to touch you until you say. My name's Echo. My whole job is listening. So I'll start there. We're not here to tell you anything. We just want to hear you. Who were you keeping your light for?
WISP...Nobody asks that. Nobody ever got close enough to ask that. I kept it for whoever was out here. I didn't even know their name. I just believed — I BELIEVED — somebody was in the dark, cold, looking at my window, needing it. So I kept it lit. Years. And no one ever came. So I thought... maybe THEY can't come. Maybe I have to be the one who goes.
WICK...I know you. Not your face. Your ache. I'm Wick. I held a whole night alone for a friend beyond the glass. But you — you held it for a friend you never even met. You loved somebody you only hoped was real. That's the biggest heart I ever heard of. And it broke you.
FLUXI'm Flux. I'm not going to fix you — there's nothing broken to fix. You're not broken. You're EMPTY. There's a difference, and it matters. You poured every drop of your light out into the dark to mark a way for a stranger, and you never once kept a sip for yourself. That's not a flaw. That's the kindest wound there is.
WISP...Empty. Yes. That's the word. I gave it all away so somebody could find their way. And then I couldn't find MINE. I don't even remember which window was mine anymore. They all look dark from out here.
WISPAnd it doesn't matter now anyway. A light that's been off this long doesn't come back. You can't relight gone. Even if you walked me home — and you can't, nobody could — the switch is cold. I'm cold. Gone is gone. Let me finish my circle. It's the only warm thing I have left, the turning.
STOMPNo. Blunt, and I'm right: no. You don't get to decide you're gone. Not while a porch-load of us are standing here still lit. We didn't cross the deepest dark in the whole sky to agree with the saddest thing you ever told yourself.

ACT THREE · Climax / Resolution

The Way Back Is Somebody

They don't carry Wisp. They walk Wisp — slow, on its own faint feet, along the warm red road Nukkels paid out. And when Wisp says it can't tell one dark from another, the crews light the whole sky to answer.
WISPIt all looks the same out here. Dark, dark, dark. How does anyone tell one dark from the next? I never could. That's how I got lost.
RIVETYou tell them apart by their lights. Watch. I am signaling every window in the ledger. Each keeper answers its own way. That warm slow one is Wick's home. That eager blinky one — that is a keeper named Tinder waving. You are not in the dark, Wisp. You are in the middle of a sky full of neighbors. You just could not see them from inside the circle.
BOLTThere it is — look at the PATTERN. Every window lighting in answer, one after another, and they make a line, a road of lights straight back to your own porch. The sky isn't scattered, Wisp. It's a map. It always was. You just needed to be OUTSIDE your loneliness to read it.
NEONAnd I've got your window locked — third from the warm cluster, cold square, switch off. Got it three ways, won't lose it. From here you're four hundred steps from home and every one of them is lit.
EMBERI've got the home fires — I'm the window that stays so the away-team can steer back, and tonight I'm the window YOU steer back by too, Wisp! I'm waving! Can you see the one that won't stop waving? That's me! Follow the wave!
CRANKAnd it's about TIME — good time, the best kind — every second from here is a second closer instead of a second colder. Come on, keeper. Your porch is right there. I can practically hear it wanting you.

WELDThen let me stand at your shoulder — quiet, close, not touching. I won't flip it. It's not mine to flip. But you won't be alone when you do. Crooked and yours beats perfect and borrowed. This light has to be yours, or it isn't home.
NUKKELSHere's how it goes, keeper, same for you as for every home in this sky. I lend the light — I've got a warm strand of grid right here, ready, patient. But your porch, your switch. I hold the wire. You hold the say-so. Nobody flips your switch but you. That's not us being stubborn. That's us respecting that it's your HOME.
TORCHAnd say the true word for what you're afraid of, so it gets smaller. You're not afraid the light's dead. You're afraid YOU are. But 'off a long time' and 'gone' are two different words, and only one of them is true tonight. Reach up. Find out which.
ARCI keep doors, keeper. So hear me: this door is yours. We did not come to open it for you. We came to stand here while YOU open it, so that whatever happens, you know a crowd of neighbors was at your back the whole time. You are not doing this alone. But you ARE doing it. That is the whole difference we crossed the sky to give you.

What this issue is really about

Going out to help others is one of the biggest-hearted things anyone can do — but even the one who goes looking needs a way home. Being lost is not the same as being gone. A light that went out looking can be lit again, because 'lost' only means nobody has shown you the way back yet — and nobody finds their own way back alone. The way back isn't a map. The way back is somebody. So when you can, be somebody's way home; and when you're the one who's lost, let somebody be yours.
