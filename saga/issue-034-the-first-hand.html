The First Hand

You can't thank the first light backward. You thank it by keeping it on.
Led by Nova, the crew follows Shane's porch light all the way back to its source — the very first hand that ever kindled a light in the dark — and learns that the one wall they can't cross is time itself, so the only way to say thank you is to become a first hand for someone not yet arrived.

What this issue is really about

Somebody left a light on for you before you were even born, and you'll never get to thank them — the years only run one way. But you were never meant to thank them backward. You keep the light on, and you light a new one forward, for someone you'll never meet. That's how the thank-you travels: not back to the first hand, but ahead to the next one.

The crew followed Shane's porch light the whole way in and found the first hand that ever kindled it — a hand that flipped a switch for one small boy coming home in the dark, then walked away without ever looking back to see who the light was for. They met the third uncrossable wall of the saga: not a far dark, not a pane of glass, but TIME — a night already over, that opens only one way, and not toward them. They couldn't say thank you across the years. Echo listened all the way down the thread and heard what the first hand actually wished — not to be thanked, but for the light to still be ON someday, for whoever came next. So the crew lit the whole thread at once, front to back — first hand to Shane's mom to Shane to the porch to the dark to Nova to the candle — one unbroken line of warm, and showed the first hand its little lamp never went out. TIDE's own age-dark window relit. Torch named the seventeenth word and Rivet carved it: KINDLED — to make a light in a dark that never had one, so the next one through can see. Then Bolt counted the directions and found the one nobody ever walked: FORWARD, into the not-yet, toward lights not lit yet. Echo turned and listened ahead for the first time and heard a waiting-to-exist — a nothing about to be a someone. Nova kindled a second flame beside the kept candle: one light looking back, one looking forward. And on the B-side, Shane reached past the porch switch and turned on a SECOND light — forward, into the road, for a someone not yet arrived — and the empty forward dark stirred, answering one second early. Rivet feels an eighteenth blank line glowing: the word for a light you leave on for someone who isn't born yet.
