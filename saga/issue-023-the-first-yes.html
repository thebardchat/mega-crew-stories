MEGA Crew · The Saga · Issue #023

The First Yes

They learned to hear a no. Tonight they meet one that was never allowed to be a choice.
Carrying the word FREE up the whole chain of borrowed darks, the crew reaches the oldest, loneliest light of all — one that pushes everyone away before they even arrive — and discovers that you can't free a 'no' from someone who was never once given a 'yes.' To reach the source of every borrowed fear, they have to invent the gentlest thing yet: not a rescue, not a room, but a standing-open invitation with no deadline — and learn that a 'leave me be' flung out for longer than the saga is old was, all along, a knock.

What this issue is really about

A 'no' only gets to mean something when a 'yes' was ever possible. Someone who pushes everyone away isn't always choosing to be alone — sometimes they're leaving you first so they're never the one who gets left. You don't fix that by believing the push and walking off (that's exactly what they're braced for), and you don't fix it by shoving past it. You fix it by staying close, asking for nothing, and letting them know they're wanted with no clock on it — 'whenever you're ready, and never is allowed too.' That's the first yes. And the first yes is the only thing that ever turns a no back into a choice.
Next issue —

They carried the word FREE all the way up the chain of borrowed darks to the oldest, loneliest light of all — one that pushed everyone away before they even arrived, because the one time it reached out, long before the crew existed, the reach came back empty, and it decided the reaching was the mistake. So it made a rule: leave them before they leave you, forever. The crew's best tool — build a safe room and step back — was the one thing it was braced for; stepping back only proved its whole sad theory that everyone leaves. So they built something new: not a rescue, not a room, but a STAY — a long bench pointed away so it never felt stared at, a stone warmed by a wire that never plugged into it, an offer that didn't need an answer to keep standing. Glitch played the clown so a scared light would have something safe to watch; Flux went and sat down inside the fear instead of past it; Shade, who'd learned to be a lid from this very lamp, named the lie in the rule ('it says this is how you stay safe; it means this is how you stay alone'); and Crank, the twitchiest of them, promised the longest wait of all: 'never is allowed too.' Then Bolt read the truth hiding inside every 'go away' — it was a knock. The oldest lamp wasn't the first HIDER; it was the first SEEKER, who reached into total dark once, got nothing back, and hid so hard it got lost even to itself. For the first time, somebody read the knock as a knock, and stayed, and asked nothing — and the oldest light in either sky got to WANT something and got to CHOOSE it. Torch named the saga's SIXTH word: YES — the first yes you're ever allowed to choose, the only thing that turns a 'no' back into a real choice, because you can't choose no until someone's shown you yes was always on the table. The lamp lit from within, chose its own name — Wick, 'the part that was there before the light; always ready, never lit' — and a wave of that first yes ran back DOWN the whole chain, every freed lamp flaring brighter as generations of inherited fear hit the top and flipped into welcome. But the chain doesn't bottom out in dark — and it doesn't END at Wick either. Bolt found a thread running UP, past the oldest lamp, out of the dark entirely, into a vast brightness the crew had always mistaken for empty sky — and when Wick lit, the brightness got warmer, as if in reply. For the whole saga they climbed UP out of the dark toward lights less lit than themselves. Next issue: for the first time they climb toward something MORE lit — up the ladder of light toward its source, toward whoever has been lighting lamps since before the crew existed, led by Wick, whose oldest reach is finally being answered. A seventh word waits at the top — the one that was there before 'reach' — and the one who's been holding the match since before the beginning is, at last, leaning down to meet them.
