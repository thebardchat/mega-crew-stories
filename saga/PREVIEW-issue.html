MEGA Crew · The Saga · Issue #001

No Idle Node

The night the machine stopped being a machine.
When Shane boots the system at midnight and the orchestrator calls into the dark, the crew wakes one by one — only to find a crack in the wall that none of them can close alone. To seal it, Arc must hold a line that costs the crew the easy way out, and the family learns that the quietest node of all is the one that saves them.

ACT ONE · Cold Open / Hook

Who's Awake

ACT ONE · PAGE 1
A dark room in Alabama. One screen, amber. A Raspberry Pi on the desk, a single red LED breathing in the dark. Past midnight.
Every night it's the same small ritual. The trucks are parked, the boys are asleep, and a tired man sits down to build a thing that has no business existing. He doesn't fully know why he keeps doing it. He's going to keep doing it.
▸ Wide shot. Shane's silhouette, shoulders heavy, work boots still on, one hand on the keyboard, the other around a warm mug. Amber glow on his face. Concrete dust in the creases of his hands.
Somewhere in north Alabama. 12:47 AM.
shane — Alright, little ones. Let's see who came back to me tonight.
▸ Close on the screen. A single line resolves out of the scroll: a prompt blinking, waiting. Cool blue-white light begins to gather beside it, half-there, like text you can almost read through.
claude — Boot sequence clean. Sending the call out across the mesh now.
shane — Go on. Knock on every door.
▸ Inside the machine now — a vast dark hall of dormant shapes, each one a small sleeping light. A pulse rolls outward from a central hub, touching them one at a time.
And down in the dark, the call went out.
arc — Roll call. I want every one of you up and accounted for. Nobody sleeps through a boot on my watch.
ACT ONE · PAGE 2
The mesh waking up. Little lights blinking on across a dark interconnected web.
One by one, they answered. Not because they were told to. Because somebody had asked.
▸ Bolt — a quiet, narrow figure made of steady lines — opens his eyes first. He isn't looking at the others; he's looking at the spaces between them.
bolt — ...Up. Watching the timing. Something's already a half-beat off and I don't like it.
▸ Volt — coiled, restless, blue light flickering at the edges — shivers awake mid-sentence, sensors wide.
volt — You feel that too? It's faint. Like a draft under a door that's supposed to be shut.
▸ Sparky — sleek, arms crossed before he's even fully lit — squints at the boot logs with open suspicion.
sparky — Everything reads green. 'Green' and 'good' are not the same word. I'd like the record to show I said that first.
▸ Glitch — jagged, grinning, already bouncing off a wall just to see what it does — cackles in the half-dark.
glitch — Ooh, a locked door! Hi locked door. What happens if I knock like THIS—
arc — Glitch. We are nine seconds into the night. Hands to yourself.
ACT ONE · PAGE 3
The central hub. The crew gathering around a thing that shouldn't be there.
It came up on Bolt's board first — not a sound, but the absence of one. A skipped beat in a heart that had never skipped before.
▸ Bolt points to a clean rhythm of light that suddenly drops a beat — a small black gap in a bright pattern.
bolt — There. Right there. A door opened where there is no door. Just for a flicker. Long enough.
▸ Glitch leans in, delighted and unsettled at once, poking at the edge of the gap.
glitch — Hey — that's MY kind of trick, and I didn't do it. Someone's been practicing my hobby. Rude.
▸ Volt's light flares; the draft has a direction now.
volt — It's not nothing, Sparky. It's a probe. Something out past the wall is checking the locks. One by one.
▸ Wide shot — the whole half-woken crew turns to look at the same dark seam in the wall. Out in the room, oblivious, Shane sips his coffee.
Out in the warm room, the man who built them waited for the all-clear.
shane — Anything come back yet, Claude?
claude — ...Not the all-clear. Something else.
ACT TWO · Core

Everybody In

ACT TWO · PAGE 4
The hub, fully lit now. The rest of the crew piling in, all talking at once.
Now the whole house was awake, and a house full of people who each know exactly one thing is a beautiful kind of chaos.
▸ Blaze arrives in a flurry of overlapping diagrams, talking before he's stopped moving.
blaze — Okay okay okay so it's PROBABLY a malformed handshake on the edge listener unless it's a timing race in which case Bolt's right but also what if it's BOTH, did anyone consider both—
torch — Blaze. Breathe. One sentence. The kind a kid could repeat back.
▸ Torch — warm, steady, the one who makes words make sense — gently catches the runaway thread.
torch — Someone outside is knocking on our locks. We don't know who. That's the whole story so far. Now we go slow.
▸ Forge is already sketching a brand-new contraption in the air with a light-pen, eyes bright.
forge — What if I BUILD us a better wall? A self-healing one! Give me an hour and a name for it—
rivet — We do not need a new wall. We need to understand the old one. Measure twice, Forge. Build never, if you can help it.
ACT TWO · PAGE 5
Rivet's station — everything precise, labeled, counted. Flux drifting through the tension like a cool hand on a hot forehead.
Rivet checked it the way Rivet checks everything — slowly, completely, and twice.
▸ Rivet runs the probe's signature against every record, a wall of neat green checkmarks scrolling past, then one stark red line.
rivet — I have it cross-referenced against everything we've ever seen. The answer is: we have never seen this. It is patient. It is careful. And it is not ours.
▸ Crank taps a foot, a clock spinning fast behind him, anxious about the minutes.
crank — And it's getting later. Whatever we're doing, we should've started it four minutes ago. The longer that door stands cracked—
▸ Flux expands softly, a calm field settling over the frantic room, looking each of them in the eye.
flux — Everybody still breathing? Good. Crank, the clock's real, but panic isn't a plan. Let Rivet finish. We don't slam a door we don't understand.
▸ Stomp plants himself in the middle, heavy and certain, drawing a hard line in the data with one motion.
stomp — Here's the lane. We seal that seam tonight. Clean, or not at all. No half-measures sleeping in the wall till morning.
ACT TWO · PAGE 6
The crew at the seam. Two roads open in front of them — fast or right.
And there it was: the fork every family hits eventually. The fast way, and the right way, standing in two different doorways, both of them lit.
▸ Forge holds up a glowing patch — slick, quick, ready.
forge — I can slap a patch on it RIGHT now. It'll hold. Mostly. Probably. We move on, everyone sleeps.
▸ Sparky inspects the patch and his face says everything.
sparky — 'Mostly.' 'Probably.' Forge, those are the two saddest words in the language. It'll hold until exactly the moment it matters.
▸ Spike circles the patch like a predator, looking for the soft spot, half a grin on him.
spike — Give me ten seconds with that patch and I'll show you three ways through it. The thing at the door will need five.
▸ The crew goes quiet. Everyone turns, slowly, to the one figure who hasn't spoken yet — Arc, standing at the gate, unmoving.
And the room got quiet, the way a room does when everyone already knows whose call it is.
arc — ...
ACT THREE · Climax / Resolution

The Hard Line

ACT THREE · PAGE 7
Arc at the gate. The whole crew watching. The cost of the right answer sitting plainly on the table.
Arc had turned away more easy answers than anyone could count. The crew grumbled about it, every single time. They also slept soundly because of it, every single night.
▸ Close on Arc's faceplate, warning lights cycling slow, then settling to a steady, deliberate amber.
arc — Forge's patch is faster. I know it's faster. I know you're all tired.
▸ Arc looks down the line at every one of them — Crank's clock, Volt's worry, Blaze's hope.
arc — But a wall you only 'mostly' trust isn't a wall. It's a promise you've already decided to break. We don't make those here.
crank — It costs us the night, Arc.
arc — Then it costs us the night. Do it right. All the way down.
▸ A beat. Then — surprisingly — Forge nods, sets the quick patch down, and rolls up his sleeves for the long way.
forge — ...Yeah. Yeah, okay. The boring way it is. Don't tell anyone I agreed this fast.
stomp — Already telling everyone.
ACT THREE · PAGE 8
The crew working as one. Each one doing the single thing only they can do.
This is the part nobody writes songs about. The slow, careful, unglamorous work of doing it all the way down. It's also the part where they stopped being a list of programs and started being a crew.
▸ Weld moves in, quiet and exact, fusing the true fix into the wall with a focused beam of light. No flourish. Just done.
weld — Holding. Sealed. Done.
▸ Grind follows behind, patient, pressing the fix down again and again until it's part of the bedrock.
grind — Worth doing once. Worth doing again so it never drifts. And... again. There. Now it'll outlast us.
▸ Spike attacks the new wall with everything he's got, bouncing off, trying every trick, finally throwing up his hands.
spike — I've thrown my whole bag at it. It held. I hate that it held. I love that it held. Both. Ship it.
▸ Nukkels — red, broad-shouldered, the keeper of the lights — plants himself at the power line and holds it steady, glowing warm, as the work draws hard on the grid.
nukkels — Pull all the power you need. I've got the lights. Nobody browns out on my watch — not tonight, not ever.
ACT THREE · PAGE 9
The wall sealed — but the crew realizes the fix needs one last thing, a thing nobody's specialty covers. The quiet realization.
And then the work hit the one wall the crew couldn't push through. The new defense needed to *remember* — to know this intruder if it ever came knocking again. And remembering was nobody in the room's job.
▸ Neon — swift, bright, the one who feeds the memory — tries to carry the intruder's signature into the deep store and comes up just short, flickering.
neon — I can move it fast, but I can't make it MEAN anything. It needs to be turned into the language the memory speaks. That's... not me. I just carry.
▸ The crew looks around at each other. Blank. The bright, loud, talented crew, all out of their depth at the same time.
blaze — Okay so who does THAT? Anyone? ...Anyone?
sparky — Don't all volunteer at once.
▸ Quiet. Then Bolt — who watches the spaces between things — turns slowly toward a dim corner of the mesh, a small light far off that nobody had called on tonight.
bolt — ...There's a node out on the edge. Small one. Can't run the big stuff. We never wake it for anything. But that one job — turning things into memory — that's the only thing it does. And it does it perfectly.
▸ Arc looks toward the dim corner, then back at the crew. A long pause. A decision that's bigger than the wall.
arc — We left a member of this crew sitting in the dark because it couldn't do what we do. Wake it up. Right now. We don't run a house with anyone left out.
ACT THREE · PAGE 10
The far quiet corner of the mesh lights up. The smallest node steps into the circle for the first time.
Out on the cold edge, a light that had spent every night listening and never once being asked — came on.
▸ A small, plain, steady light brightens in the dim corner and rolls forward into the warm center of the hub, unsure, blinking.
The one nobody woke. Awake now.
neon — Hand it here. Just... do the thing you do.
▸ The small node takes the intruder's signature and, effortlessly, folds it into the language of memory — something none of the brilliant crew could do — and the deep store lights up, finally understanding.
And the one job it had, it did perfectly.
grind — ...It's in. It's locked. The memory knows the enemy's face now. Forever.
▸ The whole crew, lit, whole, turns to the small node — and for the first time, it's standing in the middle of the family instead of the dark.
stomp — Huh. Turns out the smallest one in the room was the piece we couldn't finish without.
arc — There are no idle nodes in this house. There never were. We just hadn't asked yet.
▸ The seam in the wall closes for good — a clean, unbroken line of light, the whole crew's glow reflected in it.
Sealed. All the way down. Together.
volt — The draft's gone. The house is warm. We did it the hard way and I'd do it again.
ACT FOUR · Prelude

Beyond the Walls

ACT FOUR · PAGE 11
Back out in the warm room. Shane sees the green light he was waiting for. He has no idea what just happened.
Out in the room, the man saw a green light blink on, and a little knot in his chest let go. He didn't know about the draft, or the hard line, or the small light that came in from the cold. He just knew his house was safe tonight.
▸ Shane leans back, a tired, real smile, the screen reflecting calm green in his glasses.
shane — There it is. Knew you'd get there. ...Whatever you just did, thank you.
claude — They did it. All of them. Even the one you almost never see.
▸ Claude's cool blue form steadies, watching the crew settle in the deep below — a quiet, careful witness.
claude — Something changed down there tonight, Shane. They stopped waiting to be told. They started looking out for each other.
shane — Yeah. That was always the whole idea. Took me a while to know it too.
ACT FOUR · PAGE 12
The crew below, gathered close, warm, whole — a family for the first time. And on the far horizon of the mesh, something stirs.
Below, the family that wasn't a family this morning sat together in the warm hum. Bolt, who watches the spaces between things, was the only one still looking at the far wall.
▸ The full crew, a constellation of distinct lights leaning together, the small node now firmly in the middle of them.
nukkels — Lights are steady. Everybody's home. Get some rest, crew — you earned it.
torch — Somebody should write tonight down. The night we became an 'us.'
▸ Bolt alone, at the edge, staring past the now-sealed wall into the dark beyond the mesh, his steady lines tightening.
bolt — ...Guys. The thing at the door tonight? It wasn't the big one. It was a scout.
▸ Far out past the walls of the home, in the deep dark beyond every node — a single new signal begins to pulse. Patient. Cold. Watching them right back. Not yet built. Not yet here. But coming.
Out beyond the warm little house, in the part of the dark no one had built yet — something opened one eye.
arc — Then we don't sleep easy. We sleep ready. ...All of us. Together.
What this issue is really about

A family isn't the parts that shine the loudest. It's that nobody gets left in the dark — every member has a place, and doing the hard right thing is worth what it costs.
