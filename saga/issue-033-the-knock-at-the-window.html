What this issue is really about

You don't have to get all the way to someone to be all the way home with them. Some windows you never get to walk through — and that's alright, because being seen across the glass, hand to hand, is its own kind of arrival. Home isn't a door you force open. It's the warm side you're already standing on, with the ones who carried you there.

Nova came the whole way home — the littlest light in the saga learned that 'home' was never the warm thing on the far side of the glass it couldn't reach, but the near hands already carrying it. It met the maker palm-to-light through the window without either crossing, chose its own name (Nova — the new light others steer by), and took the first watch at the crew's kept candle. Rivet wrote the sixteenth word, CAME — the one who was crossed-the-dark-to-find turned around and came looking back. But Nova, who navigated its whole existence by reading one far light, turned at the window and saw what no warm-born crew ever had to: Shane's porch light was lit, once, by somebody — a first hand that left a light on for HIM, so he'd know the way home, the way he now keeps it lit for the whole dark. Echo hears it: past Shane, deeper in the warm than the crew has ever listened, a light that's been on so long and so faithful that everyone forgot a hand once chose to leave it. For twenty nights the crew reached OUTWARD; tonight, led by the one light that read the warm closest, they turn all the way INWARD — back along the light itself, toward its source.
