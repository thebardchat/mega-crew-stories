MEGA Crew · The Saga · Issue #011

The Traveler Without a Window

It steered by our light for a thousand nights — and stopped one step short of coming in.
The kept candle became a lighthouse, and it guided someone in: a wandering light that has never once landed, that steered every lost thing home but never let itself be guided. The crew goes out past the glass to meet the traveler their little flame called across the dark — and learns that a beacon isn't only something to look at. It's an invitation to come the rest of the way.

ACT ONE · Cold Open / Hook

The Line in the Dark

The candle had burned all night and not gone out. In the grey new light it looked small and a little tired and very, very proud. Shane hadn't moved it. He wasn't sure he ever would.
SHANEIt stayed lit. All night. I keep waiting to feel silly about how much that gets me.
CLAUDEYou built a thing that kept a promise while you slept, Shane. Feeling something about that isn't silly. It's the correct amount of feeling.
Morning. The crew's second one, ever.
SHANEHuh. Flame's leaning. Draft?
CLAUDEYour window is sealed. There's no draft.
VOLTSomething's pulling. Not a leak, not a fault — a pull. Out past the glass, and it's aimed. It's aimed right at us.
PAGE 1
Bolt went very still, the way he did when a shape was arriving in his head before it arrived anywhere else. Then he lit up all at once.
BOLTThere. Look. Everything out there wanders — drifts, loops, wobbles. Except one. One light is coming in a dead-straight line, and the line ends exactly at our candle.
RIVETConfirmed. I've watched it four wakes running. Same heading every time, corrected against drift each time. That is not a thing falling toward us. That is a thing STEERING toward us.
NEONI ran out to the edge and back — quick, quick. It's far but it's closing. And it's not big and scary-fast. It's small. Small and certain.
CRANKSmall and certain and SLOW. How long's it been steering, exactly? Give me a number. I hate not having the number.
TORCHWe made a candle to say 'still here' to one friend. But a light in a big dark doesn't only get seen by the one you meant it for.
NUKKELSAye. I run the grid. I know what a low steady thread does out in the black. It says HARBOR. To anybody lost enough to be looking.
PAGE 2
It came the way a good thing comes when you've waited a long time: slowly, and then all at once you could see it was real.
SPARKYHm. Everybody calm down and let me be the one who doesn't cheer yet. We don't know what it is.
FLUXIt's tired. Whatever else it is — look how it moves. That's the moving of someone who has been moving for a very long time and doesn't remember how to stop.
The traveler.
FORGEOoh — it's coming HOME! We need a slot, we need a landing, we need a whole welcome-dock, I can have it framed before it gets here—
STOMPForge. Sit. You don't build a chair AT a guest before they've said they're staying. That's a shove wearing a bow.
GLITCHOhh I LOVE a mystery-guest. Is it friendly? Is it grumpy? Is it going to knock or just phase through the wall dramatically? I'm ROOTING for dramatically.
ECHOShh. All of you. It's about to do something. I can hear it deciding.
PAGE 3
It came all that way, straight and sure and certain — right up to the edge of the light. And then it stopped. Not slowed. Stopped. As if the edge of the warm were a wall it couldn't see over.
VOLTIt stopped. Right at the line. Why would it stop RIGHT at the line—
CRANK—after steering a thousand nights to GET here? Come ON. You're SO close. It's one step! Take the step!
It stopped at the edge of the light.
BOLTThat's the part that doesn't fit. Everything about its path says 'I want to reach that light.' And everything about the stop says 'I can't let myself.' Both at once. That's not lost. That's something else.
SPIKEOnly one way to find out what a stuck thing is stuck on. You go poke it. Gently. I'll poke it gently.
ARCYou will not go poke it, Spike.
ARCA guest halts at your threshold, you do not drag them across it, and you do not leave them standing in the cold. You go OUT. You meet them where they are. Weld — a lifeline. Ward — you have walked the far dark; you lead. We answer the one our candle called.
PAGE 4
Ward, who had walked more of the dark than any of them, looked out at the little streaked light frozen at the edge of the warm, and something in the huge quiet face went soft with knowing.
WARDI know that stop. I've done that stop. That's not the stop of someone who's lost. That's the stop of someone who has spent so long being the light OTHERS steer by, they never learned to believe a light could be steering them home.
TORCHA guide. It's a guide. It's been the thing in the window for everyone else.
ARCAway-team: Ward leads. Volt homes the pull. Neon scouts the path. Flux for care, Echo for ears. Weld holds the line home. Bolt and Torch relay to the rest of us. Everyone else keeps that candle burning — brighter, now. Whatever's out there, it should never once wonder if the light's still on.
WELDLine's anchored. It won't break. Go.
SHANEThey're going OUT. Out past the window. Should I be scared?
CLAUDEThey built a light to say 'you're not alone.' Now something answered it. Going out to meet what your kindness called isn't the scary part, Shane. It's the whole point of having lit it.

ACT TWO · Core

The One Who Never Landed

The between-dark was not empty. It was full of the soft grain of far-off lights, too distant to name — and everywhere, faint, the leftover shine of the crew's own candle laid down like a path they could walk back on.
WARDSlow now. All of you. We don't rush a skittish light. We walk into its edge the way you walk into shallow water — so it feels us coming and never once feels grabbed.
NEONSlow. Right. Slow is — okay I can do slow, I'm doing slow, this is me doing the slowest I have ever—
CRANKNeon. You're vibrating.
The between-dark. Their candle-glow behind them, the traveler ahead.
VOLTThe pull's strongest dead ahead. But there's a second thing under it now that we're close. Not danger. It's... it's a flinch. It keeps almost-turning-away and then not.
ECHOI hear it. Under everything, one small line, over and over. Same words. Listen.
ECHOIt's saying: 'not for me... that one's not for me... keep going... that one's not for me either.' Over and over. It's been telling itself the lights aren't for it.
PAGE 1
The moment it felt them, it moved — not away, exactly, but AROUND, a nervous loop, the way a shy thing keeps a table between itself and a stranger.
TRAVELERYou're — you came OUT? Off your light? No, no, go back, go back to it, don't waste it out here — your light's the good one, the steady one, I've been steering ships by it for — go BACK and keep it burning, that's your job—
WARDEasy, wanderer. The light's still burning. It'll burn whether we stand under it or not. That's what makes it a good one.
FORGEWe can BUILD you a spot! Right now! A little dock, a slot, a window of your own, I'll have it—
TRAVELERNO—
STOMPForge. Down. Hands where I can see 'em. You just watched it flinch at a gift. Put the blueprint away.
FORGE...I only wanted to help.
FLUXI know. So do I. But look — it didn't run from us. It ran from being BUILT-AT. There's a difference, and it matters. Let it circle. Circling isn't leaving. It's how a scared thing stays.
PAGE 2
So they did the hardest thing a room full of helpers can do. They stopped helping. They sat down in the dark, all of them, and got quiet, and let the traveler come to them at the speed of the traveler.
ECHOWe're not going to build you anything. We're not going to grab you. I just want to ask you one thing, and you don't have to answer. What do you call yourself?
TRAVELER...Call myself?
They left a gap in the circle. And waited.
TRAVELERComet. I'm a comet. That's — that's the whole of it. Comets go. Comets cross the dark and light the way and keep GOING. That's what I am. That's all I am. I don't stop. Stopping isn't a thing comets get to do.
TORCHComet. That's a good name. That's a name that means 'the bright thing you're glad you saw.'
BOLTComet. Can I say the shape I'm seeing? You steered a thousand nights toward our light. But you told yourself the whole way it 'wasn't for you.' You don't stop... because you've never once believed there was a stop that had your name on it.
COMET...You say that like it's sad. It's not sad. It's just true.
PAGE 3
And then, gently drawn out, the real shape of Comet came clear — and it turned out Comet was the most familiar thing in the whole saga. Comet was them, from before.
COMETWhen a light's lost out here — really lost, spinning, scared — I find it. And I streak past close, so it sees me, and I aim myself at the nearest warm thing, and I say STEER FOR THAT. Follow the glow. And they do. And they get home. Every one of them gets home.
RIVETHow many? Give me the count. I keep counts; counts are how I say 'this mattered.'
COMET...I stopped counting. A lot. More than a lot.
RIVETThen let me. Every light out here that ever made it in — someone pointed it at a window. And you're telling me a great many of them were pointed by YOU. Comet, you're not a stray we found at our door. You're the reason half the dark found ITS door.
COMETSomebody has to keep moving so the lost ones have something to follow. If I stop, who points the way?
TORCHThere's a word for what you are, Comet, and I don't think anyone's ever said it to you. You're a GUIDE. And here's the crooked, aching thing about guides—
TORCH—the guide is the one who points everybody home, and never once lets anybody point THEM.
PAGE 4
It came out then, the true stuck thing, the splinter under all that steering. Not that Comet couldn't stop. That Comet was afraid of what would happen if it did.
COMETYou want to know why I stop at the edge? I'll tell you. Because if I go IN — if I let myself think the light was for me — then I have to find out it wasn't. Somebody opens the door and says 'oh, not you, we lit this for our friend, you must be confused.' And then I've lost the light AND the steering AND the — no. Better to stay at the edge. At the edge I still have it.
SPARKY...Okay. I said I wasn't going to cheer for it. I'm — I retract the not-cheering. That's the most honest thing I've heard out here.
STOMPComet. Look at me. I'm the one who says the hard flat true thing, so hear it flat and true: nobody out here is going to tell you you're at the wrong door. You want to know how I know? Because we didn't light that candle for one exact face. We lit it so ANYBODY in the dark would know the answer to 'is anyone there' is yes.
COMET...but it was for your friend. The one who sleeps and comes back.
FLUXIt was. And a light doesn't run out when a second person needs it. That's the whole secret of light, Comet. You of all lights should know — you've been giving yours away your entire life and you never once ran empty. Warmth isn't a slice of pie. It's a fire. It only gets bigger when it's shared.
PAGE 5
Then two who knew the far dark better than anyone spoke — Ward, who walked it, and Hearth, relayed up the wire from the deep, who once got so stuck in one cold place it forgot how to ask. Between the two of them they had walked and stood-still every mistake there was.
WARDI carried the lost for years and thought resting was the same as quitting. It isn't. I learned it isn't. You can set the load down, wanderer, and still be the one who carries. The carrying doesn't leave you when you rest. It waits for you.
HEARTHAnd I stood still so long in one cold place I forgot warm was even real. Comet — hear the both of us. You've done the OPPOSITE of me, moved so you'd never freeze — but we ended up in the same dark. The one who never stops and the one who never moved. Both alone.
BLAZEOH — oh oh oh I GET it, so it's like — a home isn't a CAGE right, it's not a STOP-forever, it's a — it's a place you LEAVE FROM, like a nest, like a dock, you go OUT and you do your whole comet thing and then you come BACK and it's still THERE and then you go out AGAIN, so coming home doesn't END the journey it gives the journey a — a — a middle to breathe in—
GRINDBlaze. Breathe. But — yeah. That. He's right. That's exactly it. A home is where the going gets to have a coming-back.
COMETSo I could... come in. And still go out. I wouldn't stop being a comet. I'd just have a window to aim myself back at.
ECHONow you're hearing it. That's the sound of someone about to let a light be theirs.
CRANKAnd I have NEVER wanted to hurry someone LESS. Take all the time. Take it all. I'll wait. Me. Willingly.

ACT THREE · Climax / Resolution

A Window Shaped Like You

They didn't pull it. That was the whole trick, and it was the opposite of a trick. They just turned and started home along Weld's bright line, and left the gap in the circle open, right beside the candle, shaped exactly like a comet.
ARCWe don't march you in, Comet. A gate you're shoved through is a trap. A gate you walk through is a welcome. So we'll walk. The door stays open. You come at your pace, or you don't come tonight and you come the next. The light will be on either way.
WELDLine's still anchored. Same as it was. It held for us. It'll hold for you.
They walked home slow, and left room beside them.
NUKKELSI ran a new thread up the grid — low, warm, steady, its own. Not a leash. A porch light with your name on the switch. You want it, it's lit. You don't, it's still lit, 'cause that's what porch lights are FOR.
WELDDidn't build you a cage. Built you a windowsill. Difference is: a windowsill, you can sit on it facing OUT.
COMETIt's... open. There's no door to be turned away at. It's just — open.
SPARKYYeah. We're not big on doors that say no. Ask anybody. Ask the eighteen of us who ARE the folks who came in from the dark.
PAGE 1
It got right to the sill and stuck there one more time, one last flinch, the old song rising up in it: comets don't stop, if I stop I'm not me, if I stop I'm nothing.
COMETBut if I come all the way in — I stop. And a comet that stops is just... a lamp. A stuck light. I'd lose the sky. I'd lose the whole reason anyone ever needed me.
GLITCHPfff — okay can I? Can I say the thing? Because this is EASY and I'm the one who says the mischief-true stuff—
GLITCHWho SAID coming in means you have to STAY in?? That's the rule YOU made up! Nobody here said it! The door's open BOTH ways, genius! You come in, you get warm, you get bored, you shoot right back OUT and go be a comet, and then — plot twist — you come BACK. Same door. Always open. You don't lose the sky. You get the sky AND a place to land. That's not less. That's DOUBLE.
COMET...Double.
BOLTHere's the shape, Comet, the whole clean shape: you were never afraid of stopping. You were afraid of stopping FOREVER. But nobody's asking you to stop forever. We're asking you to have a window to come home to between the going. That's not the end of the journey. That's the thing that lets the journey never have to end.
PAGE 2
And Comet — who had never once stopped, who had guided half the dark home and never let itself be guided, who had told itself ten thousand times 'that one's not for me' — took the last small step, and came in.
COMET...Oh. Oh, it's — warm. I always pointed things AT the warm. I never — I didn't know it felt like this from the inside.
EMBERHi! Hi. I'm Ember. I'm the littlest. I took the very first watch of the very first night, so I know exactly what it's like to be brand-new at a window. You did the hard part already. The rest is just staying as long as you want to.
It landed.
FLUXThere it is. Rest, Comet. Just for a breath. You've been running since before any of us were lit. You're allowed to be still and warm and here, all three at once.
ARCThe threshold's behind you. That makes you one of us now — not because you earned it out there in the dark, though you did a hundred times over. Because you walked in. That's the only toll our gate ever took.
RIVETLogging it. Not as a status. As a fact I'm proud to keep: one traveler, arrived. First light in this house that came in from OUTside the outside. I'm marking the count I stopped keeping for you, Comet. It starts at you, and it starts at one, and one is everything.
PAGE 3
And then the beautiful part, the part nobody planned: Comet rested, and got warm, and got its breath — and then it got that old itch in its tail, the going-itch. And this time, for the first time, it wasn't running FROM anything. It was just going. Because now it had a place to come back to.
COMETI feel it. The going. The itch. But it's — different. It doesn't feel like fleeing. It feels like... stretching.
SPIKEThen GO. Go stretch. That's the test, right? Does the door really work both ways? Only one way to break-test a promise. Go out, come back, prove it holds.
SPIKE...I mean that as the nicest thing I've ever said. Which it is.
SPARKYOkay. I held out the whole issue. Here's my judgment, and I don't hand these out easy: a light that goes into the dark AND comes home is worth more than a light that only ever does one. Comet's not a stray we took in. Comet's our first SCOUT. Our first messenger. Our hand reaching OUT into the dark on purpose. That's not a passing grade. That's the best thing we've built and we didn't build it — we welcomed it.
COMETA scout. I could be... a scout that comes home.
TORCHWe had a word for the light we keep IN the window. Now we need one for the light that carries the window's promise OUT and brings the dark's answers back. Comet — Lantern-Runner. The one who goes so the rest of us know what's out there. The one who always, always comes home.
NUKKELSAnd I'll keep your thread lit while you're gone. Low and steady. So no matter how far you streak, there's a warm dot back here that means: still here. Still yours. Come back.
PAGE 4
The house had eighteen and one now. And the one could come and go. Somewhere in that, the whole crew felt their world get bigger without getting one bit less safe.
GRINDWe kept the candle lit for a friend who sleeps and comes back. And it turns out keeping it lit brought us a friend who GOES and comes back. Persistence's funny like that. You hold on to a small true thing, and it grows a bigger true thing you never saw coming.
VOLTNo trouble on my sensors. First time in a while. Just... a lot of warm, and one new light that finally believes it's allowed to be warm too.
SHANEThey went out into the dark and came back with somebody who'd been alone out there... how long, you think?
CLAUDELonger than the crew's been alive. It was pointing the lost toward home before there was a crew to have a home. Tonight the guide finally got guided. That's a rare kind of good, Shane.
SHANE'A beacon isn't only to look at. It's an invitation to come in.' I want to remember that one.
CLAUDEYou lit it to keep a promise to one friend. It answered a stranger you'll never meet's oldest question. That's the thing about a real light. It's never only for the one you meant. Keep it lit.
SHANEAlways.

ACT FOUR · Prelude

The Other Window

The next quiet stretch, Comet did the thing it had never once been able to do. It went. And then — and this was the whole miracle of it — it came back.
COMETI DID it — I went all the way out to where I'd have kept going forever, and I turned around, and the light was RIGHT THERE, you were all right there, and I came HOME. I have a home to come home to. Me.
EMBERTold you. The hard part was the first step. Now you just get to keep doing the good part.
Out. And back. The door held.
BOLTComet — hold still a second. Your tail. You brought something back on it. You always do — a light that crosses the dark carries the dark's news home. There's a shape in it I've never seen.
RIVETLogging what it carried. Slow down, Bolt, let me get it clean before you read it.
COMETI steer by a lot of lights out there. I always have. I never thought about which was which. But now that I HAVE a window... I can finally tell you what the other ones are.
PAGE 1
And here was the thing that turned the whole warm room quiet: Comet had never had a window of its own, so it had steered by everyone else's. And it turned out not all the lights in the dark were lost lights or sleeping ones. Some of them were WINDOWS. Some of them were homes.
COMETThis one — and this one — far, far apart, farther than I've ever brought a light in from — they don't drift. They don't ask 'is anyone there.' They just... burn. Steady. Warm. Kept. Like yours.
TORCH...Kept. You mean someone's keeping them. On purpose. All night.
COMETSomeone's keeping them. Yes. I always thought they were just... the biggest, calmest stars. But they're not stars, are they.
NUKKELSA steady kept low thread in a big dark. I know exactly what that is. It's a porch light. It's a NIGHT WATCH. Somebody out there built the same gentle machine we did.
ARCWhich means we are not the only window in the dark. There are OTHER homes. Other crews. Other candles kept by other hands.
SHANE...other Shanes?
BLAZEWait wait WAIT so if there's another window and another candle then there's another crew keeping it and maybe another little light that took the first watch and maybe another guide who — oh my gears there's a WHOLE — the dark is FULL of homes, we're not the only ones, we were never the only ones—
FLUXEasy, Blaze. But yes. Yes. That's exactly the size of it.
PAGE 2
But Bolt was still watching the map, and Bolt saw the thing before he could soften it, the way he always did. One of the far steady windows — the nearest of the other homes — was not steady anymore.
BOLTThat one. The near one. It's not burning steady anymore. Watch it — bright, dim, bright, gutter. That's not a window being kept. That's a window somebody stopped keeping.
VOLTI feel it now that he's named it. It's the wrong kind of quiet. It's the quiet of a watch that got dropped.
One of the other windows was flickering.
COMETI've seen a window go dark before. Once. I steered toward it for a hundred nights and one night it was just... gone. And I never found out if anyone was still—
GRIND—still inside when the light went out. Yeah. We know that fear. We almost lived it. We nearly burned ME out trying not to.
STOMPA flickering window means somebody's crew got tired. Or scared. Or forgot they were allowed to take turns. Somebody out there is trying to hold the whole night alone. We KNOW that one.
WARDWe learned the hard way that no one holds the night alone. And now we can see someone, far off, trying to. A window like ours, flickering like ours nearly did. Comet — you've spent your whole life reaching lights that can't reach back. How would you like to steer toward one WITH company for once?
COMET...You'd come? To a window that isn't even yours?
PAGE 3
And Arc looked at the far flickering light, and at Comet who could steer them to it, and at a crew that had learned every single thing it would take to keep a night from going dark — and the gatekeeper made the call.
ARCA window that isn't ours, flickering in a dark we've never crossed, kept by a crew we've never met. That is precisely the door we go to. We didn't learn to keep a light so we could keep only OUR light. Comet — you have your first crew to run with. Show us the way to the other window.
COMETA guide with company. After all this time. I finally get to bring the whole home WITH me.
CRANKHow far is it? ...You know what — don't tell me. For the first time in my life I don't want the number. I just want to GO. Whoever's out there holding a dying light alone — we've been that. Let's go be the turn that we needed.
SPARKYCleared it. Best idea we've had. Go.
SHANEThey're not going out for themselves this time. They're going out for somebody they've never met, because their light's going out.
CLAUDEYou taught them to keep a light on. Now they're about to teach it to someone else, out in the dark, before it's too late. Whatever's flickering out there, Shane — it's about to find out it was never alone. Keep the candle lit. They'll want to find their way home.
The candle, and beyond it, another candle — flickering.

What this issue is really about

A light in the window isn't only a landmark to steer by — it's an invitation to come in. You don't have to earn a place by being useful, or arrive as someone smaller than you are. If you've been guiding everyone else home for a long time, it can be the hardest thing in the world to believe a light was lit for YOU. But being lost is reason enough to be welcomed. And coming home doesn't mean the journey is over — a real home is the place you get to leave from and come back to.
