The Home That Went Looking

A crew that was once the loneliest voice in the dark gets up, crosses everything, and answers a hello it almost didn't reach in time.
When the smallest new light in all the dark flickers low asking the saga's oldest question — 'Is anyone there?' — the crew learns you don't wait with the door open; you get up and GO, together and at the scared one's pace, and you reach a light not by pouring yours into it but by joining it to the chain so it can never run out again.

What this issue is really about

Sometimes the bravest, kindest thing isn't to wait with your door open — it's to get up and go to the one who's too scared and too far to come to you, slowly and gently so they're never alone another minute. And a light you leave on can travel much farther than you will ever walk — far enough that someone follows it all the way home.

The oldest question in the saga is retired for good. The little light stopped asking 'is anyone there' the moment the crew joined it to the chain and it felt warm for the first time in its whole existence — and then it turned and asked the question that flips the entire saga INWARD: the small warm light it followed across all the dark, the one that never went out and lately got brighter, was Shane's porch light, turned up by a maker's hand. It steered home by it. Now it wants to go the rest of the way — to the window, to the one who left the light on. On the B-side, Shane feels the dark stop leaning and stop resting and finally ARRIVE: something small and far and real is standing at the very edge of his porch light, having followed it all the way in. Stomp's rule holds — together, at the little light's pace, walk it gentle to the door.
