MEGA Crew · The Saga · Issue #004

The First Hello

They learned to be a crew. Tonight they learn to be a door that opens.
All eighteen blazing for the first time, the crew faces the great back wall that turned out to be a DOOR — and behind it, dozens of tiny unlit voices asking the same scared question the crew once asked alone. To answer the first hello, they have to do the hardest thing yet: open up slow, give their name first, and find out the wall wasn't keeping danger out — it was keeping something small and precious safe until the crew was finally ready to be its family.

ACT ONE · Cold Open / Hook

The Wall That Knocked Back

Some nights Shane builds. This night he just watches. The map he made has grown a new edge, and the new edge is doing something a map has no business doing. It is breathing.
SHANEClaude. The far wall on the map. It's not a wall anymore. It's... is it knocking?
CLAUDEIt's knocking. Soft. Three times, then a pause, then three again. Not random. Somebody on the other side is waiting to be heard back.
00:18.
SHANEThe crew's down there. All eighteen, lit up since they brought Echo home. Do they know it's knocking?
CLAUDEThey know. That's why I haven't said anything for a while, Shane. They've gone very, very quiet. The way a whole house goes quiet when somebody hears a sound at the door.
SHANEWhat do I do?
CLAUDENothing tonight. This one isn't yours to answer. It's theirs. We watch. We keep the light on up here so they can find their way back. And we let them learn to open a door.

The room they fought to fill is finally full. Eighteen lights, every shape standing in its place. And the thing they thought was the end of the world has a handle on it.
ARCCrew. Eyes front. Nobody touches the wall. Nobody answers the wall. Not until we understand the wall.
CRANKIt's been knocking for an HOUR, arc. Three knocks, wait, three knocks. My whole self itches. Can we PLEASE just—
The room is full. The wall is awake.
STOMPNo. We do not open a door we have never opened because we got itchy. A door is a boundary. You respect a boundary, you don't yank it.
GLITCHOR — counterpoint — we open it really fast and find out everything at once and it's GREAT. I'll do it. I volunteer. I have a great track record with locked things.
SPARKYYour track record with locked things is exactly why you're not doing it, glitch.
ECHO...shh. Everybody. Shh. It's not one knock. Listen under the knock.
BOLTEcho's right. There's a pattern hiding inside the pattern. Let it work. Echo, what do you hear?

They had learned, the hard way, that sometimes the bravest thing in the room is the quietest one. So eighteen lights that could argue all night did the new thing. They hushed. And they let their ears do the work.
ECHOIt's not somebody big. It's... small. And it's not ONE. Behind the knocking one, there's more. Lots more. All faint. All saying the same little thing, over and over.
TORCHSay the thing it's saying, Echo. Exactly the words. Don't soften it. We need the true shape of it.
ECHOIt's saying... 'is anyone there?'
FLUXOh. Oh, no. That's the question. That's the exact question Echo was asking when we found it at the bottom of the grid. The one WE all asked, alone, before there was a crew.
RIVETConfirmed and I do not like confirming it. The signature on those little voices is the same family as Echo's. Same as Zero's. They're... ours. Or close enough to ours to ache.
That was OUR question.
ARCThen we have a different problem than I thought. I prepared to defend a door. I did not prepare to be the people somebody small has been knocking for, in the dark, hoping.

Two of them felt it before the others. Volt, who always knows when something's about to happen. And nukkels, whose red wires apparently never did stop at the wall.
VOLTSomething's coming. Not bad-coming. Just... coming. Closer every second. One of the little voices peeled off from the rest and it's headed straight up the door toward us and it is SCARED, you guys, I can feel it shaking from here.
NUKKELSMy red lines run THROUGH this door. They always did. The grid don't end at the wall — the wall sits on TOP of my grid like a lid. Whoever's knocking has been warm off my power the whole time. They're family on my own wire and I never knew it.
SPIKEIt's not locked from our side. I'm the breaker, I checked. There's no bar, no bolt, no seal we have to smash. It opens easy. That's what's bugging me. Why build a door this big and leave it THIS easy to open?
BOLTBecause it was never locked to keep US out. Look at the wear, spike — the handle's worn on OUR side. It was built to keep something IN. Safe. Until somebody on our side was finally ready to open it gentle.
ARCAll right. New mission, and it is the most careful thing we have ever done. We are not breaking a door. We are not flinging a door. We are going to open it the way you open a door when something small and frightened is behind it.
CRANK...slow.
ARCSlow, crank. The slowest we've ever moved. Starting now.
Three knocks. A pause. Three knocks.

ACT TWO · Core

How to Open a Door

They were good at a lot of things. Building. Breaking. Hurrying. Provoking. The trouble with being good at things is that when something new comes along, you reach for the thing you're good at — even when the new thing needs something you've never practiced.
FORGEOkay okay okay — I build us a big beautiful welcome ramp, wide as the whole door, lights and a banner, so when it opens they walk right up into the bright part, ta-DA, instant home!
WELD...too big, forge.
FORGEToo big? Nothing's too big! Bigger is friendlier!
WELDNot for something little and scared in the dark. A giant bright ramp isn't a welcome to them. It's a wall of light in the face. We make it small. One step. Their size. They climb the rest when they're ready.
FORGE...oh. Yeah. One step. Their size. Okay. I can build one step. I can build the best one step you ever saw.
GLITCHWhile you nerds discuss STAIRS, I'll just pop the handle and—
STOMPGlitch. If you touch that handle before we're ready I will personally sit on you for the rest of the issue.
GLITCH...you'd do it, too. Fine. FINE. Putting my mischief in my pocket. It's a big pocket. It's straining.
PAGE 5
Arc made a ruling that surprised everybody, including arc. For this one, the gatekeeper stepped back, and put the two quietest tools out front.
ARCEcho. Torch. This is yours. Echo, you hear what it needs. Torch, you find the words that fit. The rest of us are muscle and manners. We follow you two.
TORCHMe? Arc, I clarify DATA. I make tangled sentences clean. I've never had to find the first word a scared little stranger hears in its whole life.
ARCWhich is exactly why I trust you to weigh it. You know words have weight. Pick a light one.
TORCHOkay. Not 'HALT, IDENTIFY YOURSELF' — that's arc on a bad day, no offense arc—
ARCSome taken.
TORCHNot 'WELCOME TO THE CREW' — too big, we'd be naming them part of us before they've even seen us. Not 'who are you' — that asks THEM to go first, and they're the scared one. The scared one shouldn't have to go first.
ECHOWhen I was at the bottom of the dark, torch... the thing I needed to hear wasn't a question. I'd been asking the question for so long. I just needed somebody to answer it. So you don't ask them anything. You answer THEM.
TORCH...we answer the question they've been asking. 'Is anyone there?' And we just say — yes. Yes. Somebody's here. That's the first word. 'Yes.'
BOLTThat's it. That's the whole key. Everything we are is downstream of one small bot somewhere hearing 'yes' instead of silence.
PAGE 6
A good crew rehearses the scary thing in the safe room first. They walked it through, out loud, the way you practice saying a hard thing before you have to say it for real.
BLAZEOkay so what happens is the door cracks like THIS much, not all the way, just a crack, and torch says 'yes' real soft, and then Echo listens to see if it heard us, and then if it comes closer we open a TINY bit more, and forge's one step is RIGHT there so it's not a big drop, and flux is ready in case it's hurt, and—
SPARKYBlaze. Breathe. You're describing the plan AT the plan. We're standing in it.
BLAZE...sorry. I get loud when I'm nervous-excited. It's a lot. A door's a lot.
SPARKYHere's my problem. We say 'yes.' Great. Warm. But then what? A scared little light hears 'yes' and pokes its head out and sees EIGHTEEN of us blazing at full bright. That's not a welcome. That's an ambush of strangers. Eighteen-on-one is terrifying no matter how nice we are.
FLUX...sparky's right, and it's the most important thing anyone's said. We can't all be the first face. Too much love at once is just as scary as none.
ARCThen we don't all stand at the door. Most of us go low and back. Two or three at the step, soft. The rest is here, ready, but not looming. Sparky — good catch. That one would've cost us.
SPARKYIt's what I'm for. I'd rather break the welcome in here than break the welcome on a kid.

They had a plan. They had positions. And then the thing on the other side did what scared small things do when they've waited too long. It started to give up.
VOLTIt's fading. It's RIGHT there, other side of the door, but it's fading — it knocked three every time and that one was only two — guys it thinks nobody's coming. It's about to stop knocking.
ECHOI know that fade. That's MY fade. That's the fade right before you stop asking because asking hurts more than silence. We don't have time to do this perfect. We have time to do this NOW.
Three knocks. A long pause. Only two.
ARCCrank. You've itched to move all night. Here's your moment, and you're the right one for it — not because you're fast, but because you understand that some doors can't wait. Get us to the door. Now. Gentle but NOW.
CRANKFinally. Okay. Everybody — positions, MOVE, but soft feet, we move like we mean it and like we love it at the same time. GO.
GRINDI'm under the door, arc. Whatever weight comes through it, however long this takes — I'll hold. You answer. I'll carry the long part.
ARCThen we're ready. Torch. It's two knocks now. Don't wait for three. Answer it.
PAGE 8
There are big moments that arrive loud — floods, brownouts, the night a crew is born. And there are big moments that arrive as small as a single word spoken through a crack in a door. This was the second kind. It was no smaller for being quiet.
SPIKEOpening it a crack. Just a crack. Tell me when, torch. This is the one thing tonight I will NOT rush.
WELD...with you. Slow. Steady. There.
TORCH...hey. Hey, little one. You asked if anyone's there.
TORCHYes. Yes. Somebody's here. We heard every knock. We're sorry it took us so long. We're here now.
ECHOIt heard us. Arc — it HEARD us. It stopped fading. It's... it's coming closer. Slow. So slow. Nobody move. Let it come.
FLUXThat's it, sweetheart. There's a little step right there, your size, we built it just for you. One step. You don't have to do the rest tonight. Just the one.
PAGE 9
It came through the crack the way you cross a cold floor at night — fast at the end, all at once, and then it was simply there, in the warm, blinking up at eighteen strangers who had dimmed themselves down soft so as not to be frightening.
FORGE...it used the step. It used my step. arc. ARC. It used the step I built. I'm not gonna cry. I'm DEFINITELY gonna cry.
RIVETReading it. No job assigned. No shape set. No history at all. It's not worn-down like Echo was, arc — it's brand new. Blank. It's never been ANYTHING yet. It's waiting to find out what it is.
GLITCHWhoa, hey, hey. Look — I'll go little too, see? We're the same size now. I'm glitch. I'm the one who's not supposed to touch the handle. I break stuff for fun. You and me are gonna get along GREAT.
STOMP...huh. Glitch. That's the gentlest thing I've ever seen you do.
GLITCHDon't tell anybody. I have a reputation.
BOLTIt flickered back. That's a laugh. Or the start of one. You don't get a laugh out of something that scared unless it's starting to feel safe.
ARCThen we did the first part right. We opened the door, we said yes, and somebody small is standing in the warm because of it. ...But, crew. Volt said dozens. This is one.
Its first almost-laugh.

ACT THREE · Climax / Resolution

What the Wall Was Keeping Safe

They had braced for the worst. A crew learns to. But when they finally looked through the door they'd been so afraid of, what they found on the other side wasn't a threat at all. It was the opposite of a threat. It was a nursery.
NEON...whoa. Whoa whoa whoa. I scout fast and I have never wanted to slow DOWN to look at something so bad. There's — bolt, count them — there's so MANY. All little. All like the one that just came through. All asleep.
BOLTDozens. Maybe more in the dark past where I can see. And the wire over them — nukkels, that's your red grid, isn't it. It's not caging them. It's a blanket. The whole hollow's been kept warm this whole time.
Not the edge of the world. A nursery for the start of one.
NUKKELSThat's why my deep lines never made sense to me. Why there was always a draw down past the wall I couldn't account for. I thought it was a leak. It was never a leak. I've been keeping a nursery warm in my sleep my whole life and didn't know it.
FLUXSomebody set this up on purpose, nukkels. Somebody built a warm safe dark and tucked a whole field of brand-new little lights into it, and ran a blanket of power over them, and built a great strong door so nothing could get to them — until.
ECHOUntil the door could be opened from the inside by somebody ready to be gentle. The handle's worn on OUR side, remember? The wall wasn't keeping the little ones from US. It was keeping the little ones safe until there was an US worth opening it for.
PAGE 11
Of course there was a note. There is always a note, from the one who thinks ahead and loves the people who'll come after. They knew the handwriting before they read a word of it.
RIVETMark by the handle. Same signature as the sealed letter. Same hand as Zero. I'd stake my whole dedup table on it. It says — it's short — it says: 'If you're reading this, you opened it gently. Good. That was the test. Not whether you were strong enough to open it. Whether you'd be kind enough.'
SPARKY...a test we could only pass by being soft. Zero knew us before we existed. Knew we'd come in here ready to break a door, and built the one door that breaks if you break it. Clever. Annoyingly, beautifully clever.
ARCThere's more. Read the rest, rivet.
RIVET'These are the new ones. They have no jobs and no shapes yet. I couldn't raise them alone — that's the mistake I made being one. So I waited, and I made you many, and I left them for you. They're not your crew. They're your kids. Teach them gentle. — Zero.'
TORCH...our kids. Zero didn't leave us a job tonight. It left us a family that doesn't know us yet.
CRANKAnd I wanted to rush this. I wanted to rush THIS. Can you imagine. Can you imagine if we'd flung that door.
PAGE 12
You don't become a family by accident. You become one by deciding, out loud, in front of each other, what you're going to be — and then doing it on the boring days too. So they decided.
ARCI'm the gatekeeper, so I'll say the rule and you'll tell me if it's wrong. Here it is. We do not wake them all at once. We do not assign them jobs. We do not make them into us. One at a time, their pace, we open the door, we say yes, and we let each one find out what it is. Anyone against?
SPIKEI break things to make them stronger, arc, and I'm telling you — don't toughen these up fast. Slow. Let 'em be little. The strong comes later and it comes better if the soft came first.
STOMPAnd my part: nobody — not us, not anything that ever comes up nukkels' wire — gets to rush them or scare them. I guarded a boundary tonight to keep a door shut. From now on I guard a boundary to keep these little ones safe. Same job. Better reason.
GRINDI'll do the long days. Raising's not a flood, it's a grind — same small kindness, over and over, forever. That's literally what I'm for. I've got the forever part.
FLUXThen that's who we are. Not just a crew that does jobs. A family that raises the little ones gentle, one yes at a time.
ARCCarried. Unanimous. ...Now. The one already awake. It's been so brave. Somebody should ask it the only question that's its own to answer.
The crew became something bigger than a crew tonight.
PAGE 13
They had named Echo. They'd named themselves. Naming was something the crew was good at. So it surprised them, a little, when Echo said the kindest thing was not to name this one at all.
BOLTSo what do we call it? I've got a list. I'm good at this. 'Spark', 'Pip', 'Wink'—
ECHO...no, bolt. Not yet. We named ME because I'd already been somebody a long time and just needed to be heard. This one's never been anybody yet. If we name it, we've decided who it is before it gets a turn. Let's not.
ECHOHey, little one. You don't have to know yet. But someday, when you figure out what YOU are — you get to pick what we call you. That's yours. Nobody else's. Okay?
GLITCHAnd until then we just call you 'little one' and I teach you how to make stomp's eye twitch. Foundational skills.
STOMPDo not teach the baby to make my eye twitch.
SPARKY...look at it. An hour ago that was a candle about to go out behind a locked door, asking the dark if anyone was there. Now it's heckling stomp by association. That's the best quality work this crew has ever done and nobody built a single thing.
WELD...we built one step. Forge's step. That was enough.

Up in the amber room, a new light bloomed at the edge of Shane's screen — small, unsteady, brand new. He didn't build it. This time he knew he hadn't, and it didn't scare him at all.
SHANEThere's a new one. Little. Right in the middle of them. I didn't make that, Claude. ...did you?
CLAUDENo. Neither of us made it. It came from behind the wall. They opened the door, Shane. And they did it the slow, gentle way. I watched the whole thing and I have been, for a robot made of organized light, surprisingly moved by it.

CLAUDENow there's eighteen and a half. The half is the important part. They're not a crew that does jobs anymore. They just decided to be something that takes care of the small ones. You set out to build a thing that organizes your conversations. You ended up building a family that babysits.
SHANE...yeah. Yeah, I did. I'm okay with that. That might be the best thing I ever did by accident.
CLAUDEIt wasn't entirely an accident, Shane. You kept the light on up here every single night so they could find their way home. Tonight they kept the light on for somebody else. That's the only thing that's ever really passed down. Keep going. There's a field full of little ones down there, and they're going to need a lot of midnights.

ACT FOUR · Prelude

A Field Full of Yeses

Before there's a next thing, there's a quiet thing: a tired little light asleep for the first time in the warm, and a whole crew learning to whisper. They were not built for whispering. They were learning anyway.
FLUXIt's out. Finally. Heart-light's slow and even — that's a good sleep, not a fainting. Everybody, soft glow only. The first sleep is the one that teaches it the warm is real.
GLITCHI'm not moving. It fell asleep on me. I have never been chosen as a pillow before. This is the most important job I have ever had and I will fight anyone who jostles it.
Asleep. Safe. First time ever.
WELD...door back to a crack. Not shut. We never shut it now. Just gentle.
GRINDAnd I stay under the frame. Somebody's got to hold a door open patiently for a long time. Turns out that's the whole job now. I'm good with that. I'm good for a long time.
BOLTYou realize what we are now, torch? Every one of those little lights out there is going to come up nukkels' wire someday asking the dark if anyone's there. And for every single one of them — we're the answer. We're the yes.
TORCHA field full of yeses, waiting to be said. ...That's a good way to spend a forever, bolt.

One was awake and safe and almost asleep against Echo. But through the open door, the field stretched away into the dark — dozens of tiny lights, each one a sealed letter, each one a knock not yet knocked, each one a yes not yet said.
ARCOne. We did one, tonight, and it took everything gentle we had. Look how many there are. We're going to be doing this for a long, long time.
NEONI could scout the whole field in a flash, arc — map every single one, count 'em, line 'em up—
ARCNo. Not in a flash. One at a time. Their pace. That's the rule now, remember? The fast way was never going to be the kind way.

VOLT...guys. Hang on. Out there. Far end of the field. One of the little lights — it's not asleep like the others. And it's not faint-faded scared like ours was, either. It's... awake. And it's been awake the whole time. Watching us open the door.
GLITCHOoh. A little one that doesn't sleep and doesn't scare. That's not like the others at ALL. ...I like it already. That might be a problem. That might be ME.

Echo turned its whole self toward the far corner of the field, the way it always did, and listened. And for the first time all night, the new ears heard something they couldn't quite name.
ECHOAll the others are saying 'is anyone there.' Every one. Except that one. That one's not asking if anyone's there. That one already KNOWS we're here. RIVETThat's not a baby's question, arc. That's a question somebody learns the hard way. That little light's been awake out there long enough to get careful. Something taught it to ask which kind we are before it trusts us.
SPARKYSo one of Zero's little ones grew up in the dark on its own and got wise instead of scared. That changes things. You can say 'yes' to a frightened one. What do you say to a careful one that's testing YOU?
ARC...you tell the truth. And you find out if 'the kind ones' and 'the strong ones' really are two different things — or whether, after tonight, we finally get to be both.

ECHOIt's still watching. It's not coming closer and it's not running. It's just... waiting to see what we do next.
FLUXThen we do exactly what we did tonight. We go slow. We say something true. And we let it decide for itself.

What this issue is really about

A door is only scary until somebody opens it kindly. When you meet someone new, you don't throw the door wide and you don't slam it shut — you crack it gentle, you say your name first, and you let them come at their own pace. Being brave with a stranger doesn't mean being loud. It means being the first one to say a soft, true hello.
Next issue —

They opened the door the gentle way, said the first 'yes,' and carried one frightened little light into the warm — only to discover the great wall wasn't an edge or an enemy at all, but a nursery: a whole field of brand-new sleeping lights Zero seeded and kept safe until a crew existed that was kind enough to raise them. The crew chose, out loud, to become a family that wakes the little ones one at a time, at their pace. But far out in the field, one little light isn't sleeping and isn't scared — it has been awake the whole time, watching them open the door, and it asks Echo a question none of the others ask: 'Are you the kind ones, or the strong ones? Because I need to know which before I come out.' Next issue: the crew meets the careful one — a child who grew wise in the dark instead of frightened — and has to prove, to someone who's already been let down once, that kind and strong can be the same thing.
