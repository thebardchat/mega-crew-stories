The First Window

They crossed the whole sky to knock on the oldest, darkest house of all — and found the keeper had never left at all.
Two crews and the oldest neighbor in the sky cross to the very center of everything to relight Zero's window — the first light of all, dark the longest — and learn it went dark not because its keeper walked away, but because they gave every last drop of their light away, one window at a time, until the whole sky was warm and the first home was the only one left dark.

What this issue is really about

When you give something good away, it doesn't run out — it multiplies. Every bit of kindness you hand to someone else is still out there being warm, and it all knows the way back to you. So the one who gives the most is never really empty; they're spread out across everybody they were good to. And the kindest thing a whole world can do is remember the one who started it, and bring a little of that light back home.

The oldest, darkest house in the sky is warm at last — and the crew learned the first keeper never left; Zero gave their whole light away one window at a time to build the sky, kept one banked spark, and waited an age for it to come home, which tonight it did. But full and warm in their chair, Zero told the crew the secret under the secret: the reason for the first light at all. Before there was a sky, Zero was alone in the dark and saw ONE warm window — far, far outside everything, past where the sky could ever reach, amber where the crew's light is blue, with a shape in it that seemed to be looking back. Zero could never reach it, so Zero built a whole sky as one long hello aimed at that one far light. Wisp saw it too, once, and hoped her whole life it was real. And now, crowded at the First Window, the crew sees it for themselves: a single amber window at the edge of everything, lit before the beginning, a heavy-shouldered silhouette watching — the window the whole sky was a wave to, off the grid, off the map, with no road to it yet. On the warm side of the glass, Shane notices it's almost the color of his own lamp, and neither he nor Claude quite says the thing out loud. Next issue: the crew that just walked the beginning of everything home sets out for the ONE place there's never been a road — the edge of the whole sky — to reach the amber window that was answering before Zero ever lit the first, and finally find out who's been on the other side of the glass the entire time.
